### Day 1: Arrival and Old Colorado City Charm

**Morning/Afternoon: Settle In**

Fly into Colorado Springs Airport (COS), grab your rental car, and head to your home base. The key to a great week is having a comfortable spot to recharge. Instead of a cramped hotel, a well-located Colorado Springs vacation rental gives you the space to spread out, cook a few meals, and truly relax.

**Late Afternoon: Explore Old Colorado City**

Once you've dropped your bags, dive straight into the heart of the region's history. Old Colorado City was the original capital of the Colorado Territory, and its historic main street is packed with charm. Forget the tourist traps; this is where you'll find incredible art galleries, unique boutiques, and a genuinely relaxed vibe.

*   **Must-Do:** Wander down West Colorado Avenue. Pop into the Michael Garman Museum & Gallery for a look at its fascinating "Magic Town," a miniature city with incredible detail.
*   **Local Tip:** Most shops close around 5 or 6 PM, so make this your afternoon activity. The real magic happens as the restaurants and bars come to life in the evening.

**Evening: Dinner and Drinks**

You're spoiled for choice here. For a classic Italian meal in a cozy, historic setting, grab a table at Paravicini's Italian Bistro ([2802 W Colorado Ave](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=2802+W+Colorado+Ave+Colorado+Springs+CO)). My go-to order is the Veal Saltimbocca. Afterward, walk over to Mother Muff's for a casual, no-frills drink at a beloved local dive bar.

### Day 2: Walking Among Giants at Garden of the Gods

**Morning: Beat the Crowds**

Garden of the Gods is a National Natural Landmark, and for good reason. These towering sandstone fins are breathtaking, but they also draw huge crowds. The secret is to go early. Aim to be at the park by 8 AM, especially on a weekday. Start at the Visitor & Nature Center ([1805 N 30th St](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=1805+N+30th+St+Colorado+Springs+CO)) to grab a map and see the incredible view of the park with Pikes Peak as the backdrop.

From there, drive the scenic loop. Park at the main lot and walk the Perkins Central Garden Trail. It's a paved, 1.5-mile loop that is accessible and gets you right up close to the most famous formations like Kissing Camels and Sentinel Spires.

![a dramatic sunrise over the red rock formations at Garden of the Gods Colorado](/static/images/blog/colorado-springs/a-dramatic-sunrise-over-the-red-rock-formations-at-01a85a.jpg)

**Afternoon: Deeper Exploration**

Once you've done the main loop, escape the crowds by exploring the less-traveled trails. The Siamese Twins Trail is a short, easy hike that rewards you with a unique natural window framing Pikes Peak—a perfect photo op.

For lunch, pack a picnic. There are several beautiful spots to stop and eat within the park. This saves you from having to leave and re-enter, and honestly, no restaurant has a better view.

**Evening: Red Rock Canyon Open Space**

Just south of Garden of the Gods is its quieter, more rugged cousin. Red Rock Canyon Open Space is a former quarry that's been transformed into a recreational paradise. It's a local favorite for trail running, mountain biking, and dog walking. The trails here offer stunning views back towards Garden of the Gods. It’s the perfect spot for a golden-hour hike before dinner.

### Day 3: Reaching for the Summit on Pikes Peak

**All Day: America's Mountain**

Today is all about conquering the 14,115-foot Pikes Peak. You have two main options for getting to the top:

1.  **Drive the Pikes Peak Highway:** This 19-mile scenic drive is an adventure in itself, with hairpin turns and jaw-dropping views. It takes about 2-3 hours round trip, not including stops. **Important:** From late May to September, you now need a reservation to drive to the summit. Book this well in advance online.
2.  **Ride the Broadmoor Manitou and Pikes Peak Cog Railway:** This is the classic, stress-free way to ascend. The 3.5-hour round-trip journey takes you through stunning forests and above the treeline, all while you relax and enjoy the scenery. It's more expensive, but worth it if you're nervous about mountain driving.

**At the Summit:**

No matter how you get there, the view from the top is legendary. On a clear day, you can see five states. The new Summit Visitor Center is fantastic, but be prepared for the altitude. Walk slowly, drink lots of water, and don't forget to try their world-famous high-altitude donuts. It’s not a gimmick; the recipe is specially formulated to work at 14,000+ feet!

**Afternoon: Explore Manitou Springs**

After you descend, spend the rest of the afternoon in the quirky, wonderful town of Manitou Springs. Walk along the main drag, pop into the myriad of unique shops, and play some vintage games at the Penny Arcade. Be sure to sample the water from the various natural mineral springs dotted around town—each has a slightly different taste.

For dinner, The Keg Lounge in Manitou is a great spot for burgers and other comfort food in a rustic setting.

### Day 4: Wildlife, Waterfalls, and Wonders

**Morning: Cheyenne Mountain Zoo**

This is not your average zoo. Built on the side of a mountain, the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo ([4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=4250+Cheyenne+Mountain+Zoo+Rd+Colorado+Springs+CO)) is America's only mountain zoo. The layout itself is a workout, but the views are as impressive as the animals.

The absolute can't-miss experience is feeding the giraffes. The herd is one of the largest in North America, and you can get eye-to-eye with these magnificent creatures on a massive elevated boardwalk. It’s a core memory waiting to happen.

**Afternoon: The Broadmoor and Seven Falls**

After the zoo, drive just a few minutes to the historic and luxurious Broadmoor hotel. Even if you're not a guest, it's worth walking around the stunning property and lake.

From there, take the shuttle to Seven Falls. This series of seven cascading waterfalls is tucked into a 1,250-foot-wall box canyon. You can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls for incredible views or take the in-mountain elevator to the "Eagle's Nest" lookout. It's a spectacular display of nature's power.

After a big day with the kids at the zoo and Seven Falls, having a home base that’s an attraction in itself is a game-changer. The **Epic Family Home: Mini-Golf, Sauna, Hot Tub, Games** is exactly that. While the adults unwind in the sauna or hot tub, the kids can burn off their remaining energy with private mini-golf. For a different kind of epic view, the **Stunning Mountain Views, Hot Tub, Game Room** property (or 26 Westmark, as locals know the street) offers a perfect retreat for larger groups.

### Day 5: A Day Trip to the Royal Gorge

**All Day: Go West**

Ready for a change of scenery? Today involves a day trip, but it's 100% worth the drive. Head about one hour southwest to Cañon City, home of the Royal Gorge Bridge & Park ([4218 Co Rd 3A](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=4218+Co+Rd+3A+Ca%C3%B1on+City+CO)).

The main attraction is walking across one of the world's highest suspension bridges, hanging 956 feet above the Arkansas River. The views down into the gorge are both terrifying and awe-inspiring.

But the park is more than just a bridge. For the adrenaline junkies, there's the Cloudscraper Zip Line (which sends you soaring across the gorge) and the Royal Rush Skycoaster (a giant swing that launches you out over the canyon). For a more relaxed experience, the Aerial Gondola provides breathtaking views without the heart-stopping fear.

**Local Tip:** On your way back to Colorado Springs, take the scenic Gold Belt Tour Scenic Byway if you have an appropriate vehicle and are up for an adventure on unpaved roads. The Phantom Canyon Road section follows an old railroad bed and is spectacularly beautiful.

### Day 6: Downtown Discoveries and Olympic Dreams

**Morning: U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum**

Start your day in downtown Colorado Springs at the state-of-the-art U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum. This is one of the most accessible and interactive museums in the world. Even if you're not a huge sports fan, the stories of the athletes are incredibly inspiring. You can try your hand at interactive exhibits like a virtual ski jump or a 30-meter dash against an Olympian. Plan for at least 2-3 hours here.

**Afternoon: Explore Downtown Colorado Springs**

After the museum, explore the rest of downtown. Tejon Street is the main artery, lined with local shops, restaurants, and public art. Check out Poor Richard's, a beloved local institution that's a bookstore, toy store, restaurant, and wine bar all in one.

Grab lunch at Shuga's ([702 S Cascade Ave](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=702+S+Cascade+Ave+Colorado+Springs+CO)), a quirky and vibrant spot known for its amazing sandwiches and "Shuga's Grog." My favorite is the Spicy Brazilian Coconut Shrimp soup—it's legendary.

**Evening: A Toast to Your Trip**

Colorado Springs has a booming craft beverage scene. While you can find a full guide on our blog, a great place to start is Local Relic, a brewery housed in an old church that serves up unique and experimental beers. If cocktails are more your speed, The Archives offers a speakeasy vibe with expertly crafted drinks. This is the perfect way to toast to an amazing week of exploration.

### Day 7: A Local's Morning and Farewell

**Morning: Palmer Park**

For your final morning, skip the tourist icons and head to a local's favorite: Palmer Park. This geological marvel is like a miniature Garden of the Gods right in the middle of the city. Drive the scenic loop to the top for a panoramic 360-degree view of the entire Pikes Peak region. It’s the perfect place to reflect on your trip and see all the places you've been from a new perspective. The trails are sandy and fun to explore, offering one last dose of that classic Colorado landscape.

**Brunch: One Last Great Meal**

Before heading to the airport, have a fantastic farewell brunch. Urban Egg is a local chain with several locations, and their pancakes and benedicts are top-notch. For something a bit more unique, The Carter Payne is a beautiful space in a historic church that houses multiple food and drink vendors, including a fantastic brunch spot.

### Frequently Asked Questions About Visiting Colorado Springs

<br>
<details>
<summary><strong>What is the best time of year to visit Colorado Springs?</strong></summary>
While summer (June-August) is the most popular time with warm weather perfect for hiking, the shoulder seasons are a local secret. September and October offer beautiful fall colors, fewer crowds, and crisp, sunny days. Spring (April-May) can be unpredictable with late snow, but it's a great time to see the landscape come to life.
</details>

<br>
<details>
<summary><strong>Do I need to rent a car in Colorado Springs?</strong></summary>
Yes, it's highly recommended. Colorado Springs is a sprawling city, and its best attractions like Pikes Peak, Garden of the Gods, and Cheyenne Mountain Zoo are spread out. A car will give you the freedom and flexibility to follow this itinerary and explore at your own pace.
</details>

<br>
<details>
<summary><strong>How many days do you really need in Colorado Springs?</strong></summary>
While you can see the main highlights in a long weekend (3-4 days), a full week allows you to explore more deeply without feeling rushed. A 7-day trip gives you time for a day trip like the Royal Gorge, a more relaxed pace to acclimate to the altitude, and the chance to discover local favorites beyond the big-name attractions.
</details>

<br>
<details>
<summary><strong>Is Colorado Springs a good destination for families?</strong></summary>
Absolutely! With attractions like the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, the Penny Arcade in Manitou Springs, the interactive Olympic & Paralympic Museum, and countless trails for all skill levels, it's an outdoor playground for kids and adults alike. Choosing a vacation rental with amenities like a game room or yard can make the trip even more family-friendly.
</details>
