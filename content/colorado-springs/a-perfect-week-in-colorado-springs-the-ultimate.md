### <a name="day-1"></a>Day 1: Arrival and Old Colorado City Charm

**Morning/Afternoon: Settle In**

Fly into Colorado Springs Airport (COS), which is small, efficient, and wonderfully easy to navigate. Grab your rental car—you'll definitely want one for the week—and head to your home base. The key today is to take it easy. You're now at 6,035 feet, and jumping into strenuous activity is a recipe for a headache. Drink more water than you think you need, and then drink some more.

**Late Afternoon: Explore Old Colorado City**

Once you've dropped your bags, ease into your vacation with a stroll through Old Colorado City. This National Historic District was the original capital of the Colorado Territory, and its preserved 19th-century buildings now house a fantastic collection of art galleries, indie boutiques, and restaurants.

*   **Don't Miss:** Wander down West Colorado Avenue. Pop into the **Michael Garman Museum & Gallery** for a dose of whimsical, miniature Americana. For a unique souvenir, check out the handcrafted jewelry at **Eve's Revolution**.
*   **Local Tip:** Avoid the weekend crowds by visiting on a weekday afternoon. Parking can be found on the side streets, but the public lot between 25th and 26th Street is your best bet.

**Evening: Dinner and a Fire Pit**

For dinner, grab a table at **Paravicini's Italian Bistro** ([2802 W Colorado Ave](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=2802+W+Colorado+Ave+Colorado+Springs+CO)). It's a local institution with classic, hearty Italian food that's perfect after a travel day. The secret here is to ask for their seasonal ravioli; it’s never on the main menu but it's always incredible.

After dinner, head back to your rental to relax. This is where having a great outdoor space pays off. Settle in, enjoy the cool mountain air, and plan for the week ahead.

### <a name="day-2"></a>Day 2: Conquering the Garden of the Gods

**Morning: Beat the Crowds**

Today is all about Colorado Springs' crown jewel: Garden of the Gods. This National Natural Landmark is free to enter, which means it gets *busy*. The single best piece of advice is to go early. Aim to be at the park entrance by 8 AM, especially if you're visiting in the summer.

Start at the **Garden of the Gods Visitor & Nature Center** ([1805 N 30th St](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=1805+N+30th+St+Colorado+Springs+CO)). The exhibits are great, but the real reason to stop here first is for the terrace view. It perfectly frames the red rocks with Pikes Peak in the background and will help you get your bearings. Grab a map before you head into the park itself.

**Late Morning: Hiking the Central Garden**

Drive into the main park and find a spot in the main parking lot near the North entrance. From here, you can access the Perkins Central Garden Trail. It's a paved, 1.5-mile loop that is accessible and takes you right through the heart of the most famous formations like Kissing Camels and Sentinel Spires. Take your time, read the placards, and watch for rock climbers scaling the sheer faces.

![dramatic red rock formations of Garden of the Gods at sunrise](/static/images/blog/colorado-springs/dramatic-red-rock-formations-of-garden-of-the-gods-f1e8ff.jpg)

**Afternoon: Explore by Car or E-Bike**

After the main loop, hop back in your car and drive the full Juniper Way Loop. This scenic drive offers different perspectives and access to less-crowded trailheads.

*   **Pro-Tip:** For a more immersive experience, rent e-bikes from **Amp'd Adventures** right at the visitor center. It's a fantastic way to cover more ground than walking without being stuck inside a car. You can easily zip up to the Siamese Twins Trail for an incredible photo op framing Pikes Peak.

**Evening: A Casual Dinner with a View**

You'll likely be tired and hungry. Head to **Cerberus Brewing Company** ([702 W Colorado Ave](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=702+W+Colorado+Ave+Colorado+Springs+CO)) for fantastic craft beer and some of the best gastropub food in the city. Their patio is massive and dog-friendly, offering great views of the mountains you just explored. The truffle fries are non-negotiable.

### <a name="day-3"></a>Day 3: Reaching New Heights on Pikes Peak

**All Day: America's Mountain**

Today you're heading to the summit of Pikes Peak, the 14,115-foot mountain that inspired the song "America the Beautiful." You have two main options for getting to the top: driving the Pikes Peak Highway or taking the Broadmoor Manitou and Pikes Peak Cog Railway.

*   **Pikes Peak Highway:** This 19-mile paved road is a thrilling drive with hairpin turns and breathtaking (literally) views. It gives you the flexibility to stop at various scenic overlooks and alpine lakes along the way. **Crucial:** You *must* [make a reservation online](https://www.pikespeakcolorado.com/) in advance for a timed entry slot during the busy season (late May to September). The drive takes about 2-3 hours round trip, not including stops.
*   **The Cog Railway:** A more relaxing option. This historic train departs from Manitou Springs and chugs its way to the summit. It's a 3.5-hour round trip. The views are spectacular, you don't have to worry about the drive, and you get to learn about the mountain's history from the conductor. Book tickets *weeks* or even months in advance.

**At the Summit:**

Whichever way you arrive, the summit is often cold and windy, even in summer. Bring layers! The new Summit Visitor Center is fantastic. Walk around, take in the 360-degree views, and do the one thing you absolutely must do: eat a world-famous, high-altitude donut. They are made with a special recipe that only works above 14,000 feet.

**Important Altitude Note:** Don't overexert yourself at the summit. The air is thin. Walk slowly, drink water, and if you feel dizzy or nauseous, it's time to head back down.

**Evening: Well-Deserved Relaxation**

After a day on the mountain, your body will thank you for a relaxing evening. Pick up some takeout from **503W** ([503 W Colorado Ave](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=503+W+Colorado+Ave+Colorado+Springs+CO))—their Asian-fusion menu is creative and delicious—and enjoy a quiet night in.

### <a name="day-4"></a>Day 4: Quirky Manitou Springs & Subterranean Wonders

**Morning: Explore Manitou Springs**

Just west of Colorado Springs lies the wonderfully eccentric town of Manitou Springs. It has a completely different vibe—think funky art galleries, new-age shops, and a massive, old-school penny arcade.

Start your day by walking through town and trying the mineral water. There are eight publicly accessible mineral springs, each with a slightly different taste and purported health benefit. Bring a water bottle and see if you can sample them all. Then, lose an hour (and a few dollars in quarters) at the **Manitou Springs Penny Arcade**, which has hundreds of games, from vintage pinball to classic Skee-Ball.

**Afternoon: Cave of the Winds**

Just up the road from Manitou is **Cave of the Winds Mountain Park**. Take the Discovery Tour, a 45-minute guided walk through illuminated caverns full of stalactites and stalagmites. It’s a great way to escape the afternoon heat, as the cave stays a constant 54°F year-round. For the more adventurous, they offer lantern tours and a wild caving tour.

![historic Penny Arcade in Manitou Springs Colorado](/static/images/blog/colorado-springs/historic-penny-arcade-in-manitou-springs-colorado-dd4657.jpg)

**Evening: A Taste of the Wild West**

For dinner, head back toward Colorado Springs for a unique experience at **The Rabbit Hole** ([101 N Tejon St](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=101+N+Tejon+St+Colorado+Springs+CO)). This "underground" restaurant has a creative, Alice-in-Wonderland-inspired theme and a menu featuring exotic meats like bison, elk, and, yes, rabbit. The cocktails are top-notch.

### <a name="day-5"></a>Day 5: Canyon Adventures at Seven Falls & Red Rock

**Morning: The Grandest Mile**

Head south to **The Broadmoor Seven Falls** ([1 The Broadmoor](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=1+The+Broadmoor+Colorado+Springs+CO)). This series of seven cascading waterfalls is located in a stunning 1,250-foot-wall box canyon. Be prepared for some exercise: you have to climb 224 steps to get to the top of the falls. The reward is access to short hiking trails with incredible views looking back down the canyon.

*   **Insider Tip:** The falls are owned by The Broadmoor hotel. You can't park at the entrance; you must park at their designated lot and take a free shuttle. Arrive early to get on one of the first shuttles of the day.

**Afternoon: Red Rock Canyon Open Space**

After the structured beauty of Seven Falls, enjoy the freedom of **Red Rock Canyon Open Space** ([3550 W High St](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=3550+W+High+St+Colorado+Springs+CO)). This city-owned park is often called the "local's Garden of the Gods." It has similar red rock fins and formations but is much larger and less crowded, with an extensive network of trails for hiking and mountain biking. The Quarry Pass Trail offers fantastic views and a bit of a scramble. It's also a designated off-leash dog area, making it a great spot if you're traveling with your pup.

### <a name="day-6"></a>Day 6: High-Altitude Animals and Historic Grandeur

**Morning: Cheyenne Mountain Zoo**

This is not your average zoo. The **Cheyenne Mountain Zoo** is America's only mountain zoo, built on the side of Cheyenne Mountain. The enclosures are designed to mimic natural habitats, and you'll be doing a fair bit of walking up and down hills.

The absolute must-do experience is feeding the giraffes. The herd is one of the largest and most prolific in the world, and their elevated boardwalk puts you at eye-level for a truly unforgettable encounter. Also, don't miss the views from the Will Rogers Shrine of the Sun, a historic monument located further up the mountain (accessible with your zoo ticket).

**Afternoon: Explore The Broadmoor**

After the zoo, drive down the mountain to **The Broadmoor**, a legendary, five-star resort that feels like a European village. You don't have to be a guest to explore the stunning grounds. Walk around Cheyenne Lake, admire the architecture, and browse the high-end shops.

*   **Local Secret:** For a relatively affordable way to experience the hotel, grab a cocktail at **The Hotel Bar** or a coffee and pastry from **Espresso's**. It's a great way to soak in the opulent atmosphere without the five-star price tag.

**Evening: Final Dinner**

For your last big dinner in Colorado Springs, treat yourself to a meal at **The Golden Bee** ([1 Lake Ave](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=1+Lake+Ave+Colorado+Springs+CO)), the authentic 19th-century English pub at The Broadmoor. It was actually dismantled and shipped from the UK. The atmosphere is lively, with a nightly ragtime piano player and a sing-along starting at 9:30 PM. The fish and chips are classic and excellent.

### <a name="day-7"></a>Day 7: Downtown Discoveries & Departure

**Morning: U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum and Downtown**

Spend your last morning exploring downtown Colorado Springs. Start at the magnificent **U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum**. This state-of-the-art, interactive museum is a moving tribute to Team USA athletes. Even if you're not a huge sports fan, the stories and exhibits are incredibly inspiring.

After the museum, take a walk around downtown. Check out the public art in **Acacia Park**, browse the shelves at **Poor Richard's Bookstore**, and grab a final, excellent coffee from **The Wild Goose Meeting House**.

**Afternoon: Departure**

Enjoy one last look at Pikes Peak as you head back to the Colorado Springs Airport (COS) for your flight home, filled with memories of an incredible week in the mountains.

### <a name="faq"></a>Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best month to visit Colorado Springs?**
September is arguably the best month. The summer crowds have thinned, the weather is still warm and pleasant, and the fall colors begin to appear at higher elevations. However, June through August offer the most reliable weather for high-altitude activities like visiting Pikes Peak.

**How many days do you really need in Colorado Springs?**
While you can see a few highlights in a 3-day weekend, a 5 to 7-day trip is ideal. This allows you to explore the major attractions like Garden of the Gods and Pikes Peak without rushing, while also having time for places like Manitou Springs, the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, and some actual relaxation.

**Do I need to worry about altitude sickness in Colorado Springs?**
Colorado Springs is over 6,000 feet above sea level, so altitude sickness is a possibility, though most visitors only experience mild symptoms. The best way to prevent it is to take it easy on your first day, avoid strenuous exercise, limit alcohol, and drink plenty of water.

**Is Colorado Springs walkable?**
Certain neighborhoods like Downtown, Old Colorado City, and Manitou Springs are very walkable. However, to get between these areas and to major attractions like Garden of the Gods or the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, you will need a car.
