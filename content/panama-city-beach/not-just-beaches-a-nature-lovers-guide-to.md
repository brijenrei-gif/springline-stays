## St. Andrews State Park: A Coastal Paradise

St. Andrews State Park is consistently ranked as one of the best state parks in the country, and it’s not hard to see why. This 1,200-acre park, located at the far eastern tip of Panama City Beach, offers a spectacular slice of undeveloped Florida coastline. It's a place where you can experience the Gulf, the Grand Lagoon, and a rich inland ecosystem all in one visit.

The park entrance is at [4607 State Park Ln, Panama City, FL 32408](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=4607+State+Park+Ln+Panama+City+FL+32408), and there’s a small per-vehicle entrance fee that is worth every penny.

### The Jetties: Snorkeling, Fishing, and Dolphin Spotting

One of the park's main draws is "the kiddie pool," a calm, protected swimming area behind the east jetty that’s perfect for families. But the real magic is on the jetties themselves. This rocky outcrop is a hotspot for fishing and provides some of the best, most accessible snorkeling on the entire coast. On a clear day, the underwater visibility is incredible.

**Local Tip:** Walk out to the end of the jetty around sunrise or sunset. It’s one of the best places to watch the fishing charters and sailboats pass through the channel, and you’ll almost always spot pods of dolphins playing in the currents.

![large rocks of a fishing jetty in Panama City Beach](/static/images/blog/panama-city-beach/large-rocks-of-a-fishing-jetty-in-panama-city-beac-993b38.jpg)

### Gator Lake and Button Bush Marsh Overlook

Venture away from the Gulf and you’ll find the park’s wild interior. A short walk from the main parking area leads you to Gator Lake, a small freshwater lake with an observation deck. As the name implies, this is a prime spot to see alligators sunning themselves on the banks. The Button Bush Marsh Overlook is another must-see for birders. I’ve spotted everything from great blue herons and ospreys to roseate spoonbills here, especially in the early morning.

### Hiking Trails: Heron Pond and Sandy Point

St. Andrews has two primary nature trails that offer a peaceful escape:

*   **Heron Pond Trail:** This easy, half-mile loop takes you through a flatwood pine forest and past a tranquil pond. It’s a great, shaded walk to escape the midday sun.
*   **Sandy Point Trail:** This trail leads you through the dunes to a secluded stretch of beach along the Grand Lagoon. It feels a world away from the main beach and is perfect for shelling and solitude.

## The PCB Conservation Park: 2,900 Acres of Solitude

If you’re a serious hiker, trail runner, or cyclist, the Panama City Beach Conservation Park is your paradise. Located on the west end of PCB, just off Griffin Blvd at [100 Conservation Drive](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=100+Conservation+Drive+Panama+City+Beach+FL), this massive preserve is one of the area's best-kept secrets. It's a protected wilderness area featuring cypress domes and pine flatwoods, all accessible via 24 miles of well-maintained trails.

### The Trail System

The park offers 12 different trails, ranging from a short 0.6-mile loop to a challenging 11-mile trek. They are all clearly marked and color-coded. At the trailhead, you'll find a large map to help you plan your route. The trails are a mix of packed sand and dirt, with some beautiful wooden boardwalks that cross over the wetlands.

My personal favorite route is combining the green, orange, and red loops for a solid 5-mile hike that showcases the full diversity of the park’s landscape.

![wooden boardwalk trail through a Florida pine forest](/static/images/blog/panama-city-beach/wooden-boardwalk-trail-through-a-florida-pine-fore-967d1a.jpg)

### Wildlife and Birdwatching

The Conservation Park is an official stop on the Great Florida Birding Trail. The sheer number of species you can see here is astounding. Bring your binoculars and look for brown-headed nuthatches, pine warblers, and various woodpeckers. You might also spot white-tailed deer, foxes, and other native wildlife. Just remember to keep your distance and never feed the animals.

**Important Tip:** This park is beautifully wild, which means you need to be prepared. Bring more water than you think you’ll need, wear sturdy shoes, and apply bug spray, especially during the warmer months. The best times to visit are early in the morning or late in the afternoon to avoid the heat and see the most active wildlife.

## See PCB from the Water: Best Kayak & SUP Spots

Exploring Panama City Beach by kayak or stand-up paddleboard (SUP) gives you a perspective you just can’t get from the shore. The calm back bays and unique coastal dune lakes are perfect for paddling.

### Camp Helen State Park & Lake Powell

Just west of the Conservation Park lies Camp Helen State Park ([23937 Panama City Beach Pkwy](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=23937+Panama+City+Beach+Pkwy+FL)), home to Lake Powell, the largest coastal dune lake in North America. These rare lakes are found in only a handful of places on Earth. They are primarily freshwater but periodically connect with the Gulf of Mexico, creating a brackish ecosystem with a unique mix of flora and fauna.

You can rent kayaks from the camp store and spend hours exploring the placid waters of the lake. Paddling here is a serene experience, a stark contrast to the bustling energy of the main beach.

### Grand Lagoon

On the east end of the beach, the Grand Lagoon (behind St. Andrews State Park) offers miles of calm, protected water perfect for beginner paddlers. You can launch from several public boat ramps or rent from local outfitters like Adventures at Sea. It’s not uncommon to have a dolphin swim right alongside your kayak here. It’s an unforgettable experience and one of the best things to do in Panama City Beach for nature lovers.

## Paved Paths and Urban Oases: Exploring Gayle's Trails

You don't always have to go deep into the woods to enjoy the outdoors in PCB. Gayle's Trails is a fantastic network of paved, multi-use paths that crisscross the beach, connecting parks, neighborhoods, and conservation areas. It’s perfect for a leisurely bike ride, a morning jog, or a long walk.

The trails are managed by the Panama City Beach Parks and Recreation department and are exceptionally well-maintained. A great place to start is at Frank Brown Park, which has a 1.5-mile loop. From there, you can connect to trails that lead all the way to the Conservation Park or east towards Pier Park. It’s a safe and scenic way to get some exercise and explore the area without a car.

## Pro Tips for the PCB Nature Enthusiast

*   **Visit in the Shoulder Seasons:** The best time for hiking and exploring is during the fall (October-November) and spring (March-April). The weather is cooler, the humidity is lower, and the bugs are far less bothersome.
*   **Pack Accordingly:** Always bring sunscreen, a hat, insect repellent, and plenty of water. Binoculars are a must for birdwatching.
*   **Go Early or Late:** Wildlife is most active during the cooler parts of the day—dawn and dusk. You’ll see more and avoid the worst of the Florida heat.
*   **Leave No Trace:** These natural areas are precious. Pack out everything you pack in, stay on designated trails to protect fragile ecosystems, and respect the wildlife by observing from a distance.
*   **Check for Ticks:** After hiking through any wooded or grassy areas, be sure to do a thorough tick check on yourself, your kids, and your pets.

## Panama City Beach Nature Guide FAQ

### What is the best park to visit in Panama City Beach?
For a mix of stunning beaches, fishing, snorkeling, and easy nature trails, St. Andrews State Park is the best all-around park. For serious hiking, biking, and a true wilderness experience, the Panama City Beach Conservation Park is unbeatable.

### Can you see alligators in Panama City Beach?
Yes, alligators can be seen in the freshwater lakes and marshes within St. Andrews State Park and the PCB Conservation Park. The observation deck at Gator Lake in St. Andrews is a very reliable spot for sightings. Always keep a safe distance and never feed them.

### Is Panama City Beach good for hiking?
Absolutely. While known for its beaches, PCB offers excellent hiking opportunities. The Conservation Park alone has over 24 miles of trails through diverse Florida ecosystems, and state parks like St. Andrews and Camp Helen offer shorter, scenic nature trails.

### What is the best non-beach activity in PCB?
Exploring the area's natural parks is one of the best non-beach activities. Hiking the trails at the Conservation Park, kayaking on the coastal dune lakes at Camp Helen State Park, or birdwatching at St. Andrews State Park are all fantastic things to do in Panama City Beach that don't involve sitting on the sand.
