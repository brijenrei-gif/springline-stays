Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Neighborhood Guide for Every Traveler

2026-07-21 • 12 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Neighborhood Guide for Every Traveler

Picking a neighborhood in Colorado Springs isn’t just about finding a place to sleep—it’s about choosing your adventure. Do you want to wake up and walk to a historic cafe, or do you want mountain views right from your back deck? Your home base defines your entire trip, from your morning coffee run to your evening wind-down.

So, forget the generic hotel search. Let’s break down the real vibes of Colorado Springs’ key areas so you can find the perfect spot for your crew.

Old Colorado City: For History Buffs & Boutique Shoppers

Vibe: Charming, historic, and incredibly walkable. Think 19th-century brick buildings, creaky wooden floors in art galleries, and the feeling that you’ve stepped back into the Old West—but with much better food. This was the original capital of the Colorado Territory, and it still holds onto that independent, slightly rugged spirit.

You’ll love it if: You prefer strolling to driving. You want to park your car and spend your days popping into art galleries, unique gift shops, and fantastic local restaurants without having to get back on the road. It’s perfect for couples on a romantic getaway or small groups who value atmosphere and convenience.

Can’t-Miss Experiences in Old Colorado City

  • Art Walk: On the first Friday of every month (April-December), the galleries open their doors late for a festive evening of art, wine, and community. It’s the best way to see the creative side of the city.
  • Pizzeria Rustica: This isn’t just a pizza place; it’s a neighborhood institution. Tucked away in a historic building with a stunning courtyard patio, they serve authentic Neapolitan-style pizza. The Funghi pizza with a bit of their spicy oil is a game-changer. Find it at 2527 W Colorado Ave.
  • Rocky Mountain Soap Market: Step inside and get hit with the most amazing wall of scents. They make everything by hand, and it’s the perfect place to grab a unique, locally made souvenir.

Local Tip for Old Colorado City

Parking on Colorado Avenue can be a battle, especially on weekends. Circle around to the side streets like Pikes Peak Ave or Cucharras St. You’ll almost always find a spot just a block or two away from the main drag.

The Westside: For Unbeatable Mountain Access & Views

Vibe: Quiet, residential, and pressed right up against the foothills. This is where the city grid gives way to winding roads and the silhouette of Pikes Peak dominates the skyline. The Westside is less about commerce and more about nature. You’re trading bustling streets for immediate access to some of the best outdoor recreation in the country.

You’ll love it if: Your primary reason for visiting Colorado Springs is to hike, bike, and explore the iconic red rock formations. You want to be the first one on the trail at Garden of the Gods or Red Rock Canyon Open Space in the morning. This area is fantastic for active families and groups of friends who prioritize outdoor adventure over nightlife.

Can’t-Miss Experiences on the Westside

  • Garden of the Gods: This is non-negotiable. But the local secret is to go at sunrise or sunset. The crowds are gone, and the way the light hits the red rocks is pure magic. Enter via the Gateway Road entrance for the classic, jaw-dropping view.
  • Red Rock Canyon Open Space: While Garden of the Gods gets the fame, Red Rock is the local’s playground. It has a fantastic network of trails for hiking and mountain biking, an off-leash dog loop, and stunning rock formations without the same level of traffic. The trailhead is right off US-24 W.
  • Bear Creek Nature Center: A wonderful, family-friendly spot with easy, interpretive trails along the creek. It’s a perfect “rest day” activity that still gets you outside.

Local Tip for the Westside

To get that iconic photo of the Kissing Camels rock formation in Garden of the Gods without a hundred other people in your shot, take the Siamese Twins Trail. It’s a short, easy loop that offers a natural “window” through the rocks that perfectly frames the landmark.

A place like the Stunning Mountain Views, Hot Tub, Game Room home is exactly what this neighborhood is about. Downtown Colorado Springs: For Urban Explorers & Foodies

Vibe: Energetic, modern, and increasingly vibrant. Downtown is the city’s commercial and cultural hub, a clean, organized grid of streets lined with new restaurants, craft breweries, and independent shops. It’s framed by mountain views to the west, so you never forget where you are.

You’ll love it if: You want a taste of city life with your mountain adventure. You enjoy having a wide variety of dining options at your fingertips, from upscale dining to quirky food halls. It’s great for those who want to see the U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum or catch a show at the Pikes Peak Center.

Can’t-Miss Experiences Downtown

  • U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum: This is a world-class, interactive museum that is an absolute must-see, whether you’re a sports fan or not. The stories and exhibits are incredibly moving and inspiring. Plan for at least 2-3 hours here. Located at 200 S Sierra Madre St.
  • CO.A.T.I. Uprise: An old trolley barn transformed into a super cool food hall. You can get everything from Korean street food to craft cocktails to Nashville hot chicken, all under one roof. It’s the perfect solution for a group that can’t agree on what to eat.
  • Acacia Park: A lovely green space in the heart of downtown. In the summer, there are concerts and events, and in the winter, it hosts a charming outdoor ice-skating rink.

Local Tip for Downtown

While Tejon Street is the main artery, don’t be afraid to explore the side streets like Kiowa and Pikes Peak. That’s where you’ll find some of the best hidden gems, like the speakeasy-style cocktail bar, “Brooklyn’s on Boulder.”

The Broadmoor Area: For Families & Luxury Seekers

Vibe: Stately, scenic, and a bit luxurious. Centered around the legendary Broadmoor Hotel, this neighborhood is characterized by wide, tree-lined streets, large, beautiful homes, and a sense of established elegance. It’s tucked against Cheyenne Mountain, offering a feeling of secluded mountain grandeur while still being just 10 minutes from downtown.

You’ll love it if: You’re traveling with a multi-generational family or a large group and want a home that’s an experience in itself. You appreciate proximity to top-tier attractions like the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo and Seven Falls and prefer a more polished, resort-like atmosphere.

Can’t-Miss Experiences in the Broadmoor Area

  • Cheyenne Mountain Zoo: Consistently ranked one of the best zoos in the country, and for good reason. It’s built on the side of a mountain, so you get incredible city views while feeding the famous giraffe herd. Wear comfortable shoes—it’s a workout!
  • Seven Falls: A series of seven cascading waterfalls in a 1,250-foot-wall box canyon. You can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls for a breathtaking view or take the in-mountain elevator for an easier ascent. It’s a stunning natural wonder.
  • North Cheyenne Cañon Park: This city park feels like a national park. Drive up the scenic road, stopping at pull-offs to explore Helen Hunt Falls and hike the gorgeous trails. It’s completely free and offers a rugged mountain experience right in the city’s backyard.

Where to Stay in the Broadmoor Area

This area is ideal for a “destination” vacation rental—a house with so many amenities you might not want to leave. For the ultimate family trip, the Epic Family Home: Mini-Golf, Sauna, Hot Tub, Games is a showstopper. Located at 19 Clover Cir, this 11-guest property has its own private mini-golf course, a sauna, a hot tub, and a game room. It’s the perfect home base for a large group wanting to be close to the zoo and falls while having endless entertainment back at the house.

Manitou Springs: For the Quirky & Adventurous Spirit

Vibe: Bohemian, eccentric, and fiercely independent. Manitou Springs is technically its own town, but it sits right at the base of Pikes Peak, just west of Colorado Springs. It’s a canyon town with a main street full of funky boutiques, art galleries, and new-age shops. The architecture is a colorful, eclectic mix of Victorian-era buildings.

You’ll love it if: You march to the beat of your own drum. You’re looking for a unique, artsy basecamp for tackling the Manitou Incline or driving up the Pikes Peak Highway. It’s a fantastic spot for active travelers who appreciate a town with a ton of personality.

Can’t-Miss Experiences in Manitou Springs

  • The Manitou Incline: Not for the faint of heart. This is a bucket-list challenge for fitness enthusiasts—a nearly one-mile climb that gains 2,000 feet of elevation. The views (and bragging rights) are worth the burning legs. Crucial tip: You MUST make a free reservation online in advance.
  • Penny Arcade: A massive, rambling arcade that has been a fixture for generations. You’ll find everything from antique, hand-cranked games that still cost a penny to modern video games. It’s pure, nostalgic fun for all ages.
  • Taste the Mineral Springs: The town is named for the eight natural mineral springs dotted throughout. Each has a unique taste and mineral composition. Grab a cup and a map from the visitor center and take a self-guided tasting tour.

Local Tip for Manitou Springs

Parking is notoriously difficult and expensive in Manitou. The best strategy is to park for free along Manitou Avenue on the east side of town (before you get into the paid-parking chaos) and take the free shuttle bus that runs up and down the main street. It saves a ton of headache.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)


What is the best area to stay in Colorado Springs for families? The Broadmoor area is fantastic for families due to its proximity to the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo and Seven Falls. The Westside is also a great choice for active families who want easy access to hiking at Garden of the Gods and Red Rock Canyon Open Space. Both areas offer larger vacation rentals with amenities like game rooms and hot tubs.

Is it better to stay in Colorado Springs or Manitou Springs? It depends on the vibe you’re looking for. Stay in Manitou Springs if you want a quirky, walkable, bohemian town with direct access to the Incline and Pikes Peak. Choose Colorado Springs for a wider variety of dining and lodging options, and for more centralized access to all the region’s attractions, like Old Colorado City, Garden of the Gods, and Downtown.

What part of Colorado Springs is closest to Pikes Peak? Manitou Springs and the Westside of Colorado Springs are the closest areas to the Pikes Peak Highway entrance and the Cog Railway depot. Staying in these neighborhoods minimizes your drive time for a Pikes Peak adventure, allowing you to get an early start to beat the crowds.

Can you get around Colorado Springs without a car? While you can explore specific neighborhoods like Downtown or Old Colorado City on foot, a car is highly recommended to experience the full scope of what the Pikes Peak region has to offer. Major attractions like Garden of the Gods, Pikes Peak, and the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo are spread out and are most easily accessed by car.
