The Ultimate 7-Day Colorado Springs Itinerary: Adventure at 6,035 Feet

2026-10-26 • 13 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / The Ultimate 7-Day Colorado Springs Itinerary: Adventure at 6,035 Feet

So you’ve carved out a full week to explore Colorado Springs. Smart move. While you can certainly hit a highlight or two on a weekend, a seven-day trip is the perfect amount of time to truly experience the Pikes Peak region—to conquer a 14er, get lost in quirky mountain towns, and still have time to relax by a fire pit with a local craft beer.

This isn’t your average checklist. This is your day-by-day guide to an unforgettable week, packed with insider tips to help you navigate the crowds, find the best food, and feel less like a tourist and more like you’ve discovered a second home. Let’s get started.

Day 1: Arrival & Sunset at Garden of the Gods

Your first day is all about settling in and getting your first taste of the epic scenery. After you touch down at Colorado Springs Airport (COS) and pick up your rental car (you’ll definitely want one), head to your home base to drop your bags.

Once you’re settled, the first stop is non-negotiable: Garden of the Gods. But here’s the key—don’t go midday. The park is free, which means it’s packed from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., with parking spots becoming an endangered species.

Pro Tip: Aim to arrive around 4 p.m. or later. The crowds thin out, the temperature drops, and the “golden hour” light makes the red rocks glow with an almost supernatural intensity.

Start at the Garden of the Gods Visitor & Nature Center (1805 N 30th St). The exhibits are great, but the real reason you’re here is for the terrace view. It perfectly frames the entire park with Pikes Peak in the background and is one of the best photo ops in the city.

From there, drive into the park itself. You can do the main scenic loop by car, but the best way to experience it is on foot. Park in the main lot off Juniper Way Loop and walk the Perkins Central Garden Trail. It’s a paved, 1.5-mile loop that is wheelchair and stroller accessible, weaving you directly between the most iconic formations like “Kissing Camels” and “Sentinel Spires.”

For dinner, keep it simple. You’re just a five-minute drive from Old Colorado City. Head to Front Range Barbeque (2330 W Colorado Ave) for some of the best smoked meats in the state. The patio often has live music, and it’s the perfect casual spot to toast to the start of your adventure.

Day 2: Conquer America’s Mountain, Pikes Peak

Today is the day you go big. Pikes Peak, the 14,115-foot mountain that inspired the song “America the Beautiful,” looms over the city, and getting to the top is a rite of passage. You have two main options:

Option 1: Drive the Pikes Peak Highway

This is the choice for freedom and flexibility. The 19-mile paved road is a stunning journey through multiple climate zones, with plenty of pull-offs for photos of alpine lakes and marmots.

Tips for Driving: * Go Early: Aim to be at the entrance gate (5089 Pikes Peak Hwy) before 9 a.m. to avoid the worst of the traffic. * Check Conditions: The weather at the summit can be wildly different from the city. Call the pre-recorded info line at (719) 385-7325 before you go. * Fuel Up: There are no gas stations on the highway. Make sure your tank is at least half-full. * Stay Hydrated: The altitude is no joke. Bring way more water than you think you’ll need.

Option 2: The Broadmoor’s Pikes Peak Cog Railway

For a more relaxed (and historic) journey, book a seat on the world’s highest cog railway. The 3.5-hour round trip chugs you up the mountain in comfort, offering panoramic views without the stress of navigating hairpin turns. You must book tickets well in advance, especially during peak season.

Whichever way you get there, the summit is breathtaking. The new Summit Visitor Center offers 360-degree views that can stretch all the way to Kansas on a clear day. And you absolutely must get one of their world-famous high-altitude donuts. They’re made with a special recipe that only works above 14,000 feet.

After a long day of high-altitude exploring, coming back to a comfortable place to relax and recharge is essential. A private hot tub with a view is the ultimate reward.

For groups looking to unwind in style, the Stunning Mountain Views, Hot Tub, Game Room home is an incredible option. Located at 26 Westmark, it’s designed for relaxation, letting you soak your tired muscles while gazing at the very mountain range you just conquered. This Colorado Springs vacation rental makes for the perfect adventurer’s basecamp.

Day 3: Manitou Springs Magic & Ancient Dwellings

After the epic scale of Pikes Peak, today is about dialing it back and exploring the quirky, artistic town at its base: Manitou Springs. This historic town has a completely different vibe from Colorado Springs—think funky boutiques, art galleries, and a delightful dose of weirdness.

Start your morning by strolling down Manitou Avenue. The main attraction here is the Miramont Castle Museum (9 Capitol Hill Ave), a fascinating, eclectic Victorian home with nine different architectural styles.

Next, hunt for the eight natural mineral springs that give the town its name. Each spring has a slightly different mineral content and taste. You can grab a cup from the visitor center and take a self-guided tasting tour. Then, lose a few quarters and an hour of your life in the Penny Arcade, a massive collection of vintage and modern arcade games that’s fun for all ages.

For lunch, grab a slice at Savelli’s (301 Manitou Ave) or a delicious crepe from the food truck-turned-restaurant, The Creperie (4620 Pikes Peak Hwy).

In the afternoon, head just up the road to the Manitou Cliff Dwellings (10 Cliff Dwellings Rd). These are Ancestral Puebloan cliff dwellings that were relocated here from the Four Corners region in the early 1900s. Unlike national parks, you can actually walk through and explore the structures, making it a uniquely hands-on historical experience.

Day 4: Giraffes, Gorges, and Grandeur

Get ready for a day of spectacular heights and wildlife encounters. Start your morning at the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo (4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd), America’s only mountain zoo.

The layout is built right into the side of the mountain, so be prepared for a bit of a workout. The absolute can’t-miss experience is feeding the giraffes. The herd is one of the largest and most prolific in the country, and you can get eye-to-eye with these gentle giants on one of the world’s highest feeding platforms. It’s a core memory waiting to happen. Also, take the Mountaineer Sky Ride for incredible views of the city below.

After the zoo, head to The Broadmoor, a legendary five-star resort, which serves as the gateway to your next stop: The Broadmoor Seven Falls. You can’t drive directly to the falls; you must park at The Broadmoor and take a complimentary shuttle.

Seven Falls is a series of seven cascading waterfalls in a stunning 1,250-foot-wall box canyon. To get the full experience, climb the 224 steps built into the cliffside right next to the falls. The view from the top is worth the leg burn. If you’re feeling energetic, hiking trails at the top lead to Midnight Falls and Inspiration Point.

For dinner, you’re already in the perfect neighborhood. Treat yourself to a meal at Prime 25, a high-end steakhouse, or for something more casual but equally delicious, try Edelweiss Restaurant (34 E Ramona Ave) for authentic German food in a charming, old-world setting.

Day 5: Olympic History & Old West Charm

Today blends the city’s rich history with its modern identity as Olympic City USA. Start your morning exploring Old Colorado City, the original capital of the Colorado Territory. While we have a whole guide dedicated to it, here’s the quick version: park your car and just walk. Colorado Avenue is lined with historic brick buildings housing art galleries, boutiques, and excellent restaurants.

Grab a coffee and a pastry at Carnelian Coffee Co. (2221 W Colorado Ave) to fuel your stroll. Don’t miss the art galleries; they showcase a huge range of Western and contemporary art.

In the afternoon, head downtown to the U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum (200 S Sierra Madre St). This is one of the most accessible and interactive museums in the world. You’ll journey through the history of the games, see artifacts like Apolo Ohno’s skates and Michael Phelps’ swim cap, and even try your hand at virtual Olympic events. It’s an inspiring experience for sports fans and non-fans alike.

For dinner, explore the burgeoning food scene in Downtown Colorado Springs. Shuga’s (702 S Cascade Ave) is a local favorite for its quirky vibe and delicious cocktails and sandwiches—you have to try the spicy shrimp soup.

Day 6: Day Trip to the Royal Gorge Bridge

It’s time for an adventure outside the city limits. The Royal Gorge Bridge & Park (4218 Co Rd 3A), located about an hour’s drive south near Cañon City, is one of Colorado’s most awe-inspiring man-made wonders.

Suspended 956 feet above the Arkansas River, it was the highest suspension bridge in the world for over 70 years. Walking across it is a thrilling experience, with the river looking like a tiny ribbon below.

Your park admission includes a ride on the aerial gondola, which offers a different, incredible perspective of the bridge and the gorge. For the adrenaline junkies in your group, you can add on experiences like the Cloudscraper Zip Line (the highest in America) or the Royal Rush Skycoaster, which swings you out over the edge of the gorge.

Day Trip Tips: * Pack Layers: The park is exposed and can be very windy and cooler than Colorado Springs. * Plan for Half a Day: Give yourself at least 4-5 hours to experience the park without rushing. * Look for Trains: Keep an eye on the gorge floor; you might see the Royal Gorge Route Railroad chugging along the river’s edge.

You can grab lunch at the park’s cafes or head into Cañon City afterward. Bunk House Burgers (107 S 1st St) is a great local spot for a classic burger and fries before you make the scenic drive back to Colorado Springs.

Day 7: A Final Adventure & Farewell Brunch

It’s your last day, but there’s still time for one more dose of Colorado beauty. We’ll give you a few options depending on your energy level and flight time.

  • Option A (Relaxed Hike): Palmer Park. This park is a hidden gem right in the middle of the city. It has a network of easy trails that wind through unique rock formations and offer some of the best panoramic views of the entire Front Range, from Pikes Peak to the Air Force Academy. The Mesa Trail is a great, relatively flat loop.
  • Option B (Cosmic & Cool): Space Foundation Discovery Center. (4425 Arrowswest Dr). A fantastic indoor option, especially if the weather isn’t cooperating. This hands-on space and science museum is engaging for both kids and adults.
  • Option C (Final Views): Red Rock Canyon Open Space. Located just west of Old Colorado City, this is like a smaller, less-crowded version of Garden of the Gods. It’s a former quarry that’s been turned into a beautiful park with miles of trails, perfect for a final, easy hike.

Before you head to the airport, treat yourself to a farewell brunch. Urban Egg (multiple locations) is a local institution known for its pancakes and creative benedicts. Or, for something truly special, go to The Omelette Parlor (900 E Fillmore St), a city landmark that’s been serving breakfast since 1973.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the best month to visit Colorado Springs? A: September is arguably the best month. The summer crowds have thinned, the weather is still warm and pleasant, and the fall colors begin to appear at higher elevations. However, June through August offer the most reliable weather for high-altitude activities like visiting Pikes Peak.

Q: How many days do you really need in Colorado Springs? A: While you can see a few highlights in a 3-day weekend, a 5 to 7-day trip is ideal. It allows you to explore major attractions like Pikes Peak and Garden of the Gods without rushing, and also provides time for a day trip or for exploring lesser-known gems and different neighborhoods.

Q: Do I need to worry about altitude sickness in Colorado Springs? A: Colorado Springs sits at an elevation of 6,035 feet, and some visitors may experience mild symptoms of altitude sickness like headaches or shortness of breath. The key is to acclimate. Take it easy on your first day, drink lots of water (more than you think you need), and limit alcohol consumption.

Q: Is Colorado Springs a walkable city? A: Certain neighborhoods like Downtown, Old Colorado City, and Manitou Springs are very walkable. However, to get between these areas and to major attractions like Garden of the Gods or Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, you will need a car. The city is quite spread out.
