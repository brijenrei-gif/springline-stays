Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Local's Guide to the Best Neighborhoods

2026-08-01 • 12 min read

Choosing the right neighborhood in Colorado Springs can completely transform your trip. This isn’t a small town where everything is a five-minute walk away; it’s a sprawling city tucked against a massive mountain range. Where you decide to drop your bags determines whether your vacation vibe is historic and walkable, secluded and mountainous, or buzzing with urban energy.

So, instead of just picking a place with nice photos, let’s figure out which corner of “The Springs” is the perfect home base for you.

Old Colorado City & The Westside: For History Buffs & Walkable Charm

The Vibe

Forget what you know about the current downtown—Old Colorado City (or OCC, as you’ll hear it called) was the original capital of the Colorado Territory. It has that authentic, Wild West feel with historic 19th-century buildings, brick-lined sidewalks, and a main street (Colorado Avenue) that’s packed with independent art galleries, quirky boutiques, and fantastic restaurants. It’s less about high-rise buildings and more about cozy, historic charm. The surrounding Westside is a blend of Victorian homes and quiet residential streets, putting you close to the action but offering a peaceful retreat.

Best For

Couples looking for a romantic getaway, art lovers, and families who appreciate being able to walk to dinner after a long day of hiking.

What’s Nearby

  • Old Colorado City Historic District: The main draw. Spend an entire afternoon wandering in and out of shops like the Rocky Mountain Dinosaur Resource Center gift shop or grabbing a scoop at Colorado City Creamery.
  • Red Rock Canyon Open Space: Just a 5-minute drive. It’s like a smaller, less-crowded version of Garden of the Gods with amazing hiking and rock formations. The local’s secret is to hit the trails here for your morning hike to avoid the crowds at the bigger parks.
  • Garden of the Gods: A quick 10-minute drive, making it an incredibly accessible main attraction from this neighborhood.
  • Ghost Town Museum: A cool, kitschy step back in time, perfect for a family afternoon. Find it at 400 S 21st St.

Local Tips & Favorites

My absolute favorite thing to order at Paravicini’s Italian Bistro is the Veal Saltimbocca—it’s legendary. For coffee, skip the big chains and head to Carnelian Coffee Co. for a fantastic latte in a cozy setting. The secret to enjoying OCC is to visit on a weekday morning; you’ll have the sidewalks practically to yourself.

The Broadmoor Area & Cheyenne Canyon: For Luxury & Mountain Seclusion

The Vibe

Elegant, established, and nestled right at the base of Cheyenne Mountain. This neighborhood is defined by the world-famous Broadmoor Hotel, and its vibe reflects that five-star luxury. You’ll find wide, tree-lined streets, stunning homes, and an immediate sense of being “away from it all,” even though you’re just 15 minutes from downtown. It’s quieter, more residential, and the access to incredible nature is second to none.

Best For

Larger families, multi-generational groups, and anyone looking for a more upscale, peaceful retreat with plenty of space and top-tier amenities.

What’s Nearby

  • Cheyenne Mountain Zoo: Consistently ranked one of the best in the country. The elevation is no joke, but feeding the giraffes with the city sprawling below you is an unforgettable experience.
  • Seven Falls: A series of seven cascading waterfalls in a stunning box canyon, owned and operated by The Broadmoor.
  • North Cheyenne Cañon Park: This city-owned park is a local treasure and completely free. Drive up the scenic byway to Helen Hunt Falls or tackle more challenging trails like the one to St. Mary’s Falls.
  • Starsmore Visitor and Nature Center: The perfect starting point for your Cheyenne Cañon adventure, with trail maps and exhibits.

Local Tips & Favorites

You don’t have to be a guest at The Broadmoor to enjoy parts of it. Park and walk around the beautiful lake, or make a reservation at one of its restaurants like the classic Penrose Room. A lesser-known tip: a ticket to the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo also includes the drive up the Will Rogers Shrine of the Sun, a historic monument that offers arguably the best panoramic view of Colorado Springs. It’s absolutely worth the winding drive.

Downtown Colorado Springs: For Urban Energy & Nightlife

The Vibe

Modern, energetic, and increasingly vibrant. Downtown Colorado Springs is the city’s commercial and cultural hub. It’s laid out on a clean grid, making it easy to navigate. Here you’ll find the highest concentration of restaurants, bars, museums, and performing arts venues. It’s where the city’s history meets its future, with historic storefronts housing trendy eateries and tech startups.

Best For

Young couples, foodies, business travelers, and anyone who wants to be in the center of the action with plenty of nightlife options.

What’s Nearby

  • U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum: An absolute must-see. This state-of-the-art interactive museum is a world-class attraction.
  • Pikes Peak Center for the Performing Arts: Home to the Colorado Springs Philharmonic and host to touring Broadway shows and concerts.
  • Acacia Park: A central green space that hosts farmers’ markets in the summer and an ice rink in the winter.
  • CO.A.T.I. Uprise: An eclectic and trendy food hall built in an old trolley barn at 514 S Tejon St. Great for groups who can’t agree on what to eat.

Local Tips & Favorites

For a great cocktail with a view, head to Lumen8 Rooftop Social. If you’re looking for an amazing, under-the-radar dinner spot, The Rabbit Hole offers imaginative New American cuisine in a unique underground setting. Parking can be a pain on weekend evenings, so plan to use the city garages on Nevada Ave or Cascade Ave—it’s worth the couple of bucks to save the headache.

Manitou Springs: For Quirky Vibes & Epic Trail Access

The Vibe

Technically its own town, but it functions as a must-visit neighborhood for any Colorado Springs traveler. Manitou is… different. In the best way possible. It’s bohemian, eccentric, and fiercely independent. The main street is a colorful collection of candy stores, art galleries, and old-school penny arcades packed into a tight, walkable canyon. The town was founded as a health spa, and you can still taste the mineral waters from the various public fountains dotted around town.

Best For

Avid hikers, spiritual seekers, and families with kids who will go nuts for the Penny Arcade.

What’s Nearby

  • Manitou Incline: The holy grail for fitness buffs. This is a bucket-list challenge, gaining 2,000 feet of elevation in under a mile. Reservations are required!
  • Barr Trail: The most popular (and longest) hiking trail to the summit of Pikes Peak starts here.
  • The Penny Arcade: A sprawling, delightful collection of vintage and modern arcade games that will entertain for hours.
  • Cave of the Winds Mountain Park: Offers stunning cave tours and thrilling outdoor attractions like a zip line over the canyon.

Local Tips & Favorites

Don’t buy bottled water! Bring your own bottle and go on a self-guided tour of the eight mineral springs. Each one is naturally carbonated and has a distinctly different taste due to the mineral content. My personal favorite is the one at Wheeler Spring. Also, parking in Manitou is notoriously difficult, especially on summer weekends. Your best bet is to park on the edge of town and take the free shuttle bus that runs up and down the main street.

Northgate & Briargate: For Modern Comfort & Air Force Academy Access

The Vibe

This is the modern, suburban heart of Colorado Springs. If you’re looking for new construction, wide streets, and every shopping convenience you can imagine, this is your spot. It’s clean, safe, and incredibly family-friendly. While it lacks the historic character of Old Colorado City or Manitou, it makes up for it with convenience and easy access to I-25.

Best For

Families visiting for Air Force Academy events, those who prioritize modern amenities and shopping, or travelers who want a convenient base for exploring both Colorado Springs and the region north towards Denver.

What’s Nearby

  • U.S. Air Force Academy: The Visitor Center is a major attraction with its stunning Cadet Chapel and exhibits. Note: Check for current access policies before you go.
  • The Promenade Shops at Briargate: An upscale outdoor mall with stores like Apple, Pottery Barn, and Lululemon, plus great dining options.
  • Topgolf: A fun activity for the whole family or a group of friends, especially on a chilly evening.
  • Great Wolf Lodge: Even if you’re not staying there, you can sometimes get day passes for their massive indoor water park.

Local Tips & Favorites

This area has the best concentration of easy, crowd-pleasing restaurants. For a quick and healthy family meal, places like Modern Market Eatery or Noodles & Company are lifesavers. It’s also home to the only In-N-Out Burger in Southern Colorado, so if you’re from a state without one, this is your chance.

Colorado Springs Neighborhoods FAQ

What is the best area to stay in Colorado Springs for families?

For families who love nature and want a quieter retreat, the Broadmoor Area / Cheyenne Canyon is fantastic due to its proximity to the zoo and hiking. For families who prefer modern conveniences, newer homes, and easy access to shopping and restaurants, Northgate & Briargate in the northern part of the city is an excellent choice.

What is the most walkable neighborhood in Colorado Springs?

Old Colorado City is the most walkable neighborhood for tourists, with a dense main street full of shops, galleries, and restaurants. Downtown Colorado Springs is also highly walkable, offering a more urban environment with museums, bars, and performance venues.

Is it better to stay in Colorado Springs or Manitou Springs?

It depends entirely on the vacation you want. Stay in Colorado Springs (in neighborhoods like Old Colorado City or The Broadmoor) for a more central location to access all the region’s attractions. Stay in Manitou Springs if you want a quirky, bohemian, mountain-town experience and plan to focus heavily on hiking the Incline or Pikes Peak.

Do I need a car in Colorado Springs?

Yes, a car is highly recommended. Colorado Springs is very spread out, and its top attractions like Garden of the Gods, Pikes Peak, and Cheyenne Mountain Zoo are not easily reached by public transportation. A car gives you the freedom to explore the city and the surrounding natural beauty on your own schedule.
