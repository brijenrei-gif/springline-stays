Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Local's Guide to the Best Neighborhoods

2026-08-25 • 13 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Local's Guide to the Best Neighborhoods

So you’re coming to Colorado Springs. You’ve probably got a list: Pikes Peak, Garden of the Gods, maybe a hike or two. But the single biggest decision that will shape your entire trip isn’t what you’ll do, but where you’ll stay.

Picking the right neighborhood here is everything. It’s the difference between a 10-minute walk to a morning coffee and a 30-minute drive in traffic. It’s the difference between waking up to the quiet rustle of pines and the buzz of the city. Forget the generic hotel districts clustered by the interstate. To really experience the Springs, you need to choose your home base wisely.

This isn’t just another list of places. This is your guide to finding the perfect slice of Colorado Springs that feels like it was made just for you.

Old Colorado City: For the History Buffs and Boutique Shoppers

The Vibe

Step onto Colorado Avenue in Old Colorado City (OCC), and you’re stepping back in time. This was the original capital of the Colorado Territory, and it still has that rugged, historic charm. Think brick-lined sidewalks, Victorian architecture, and a fierce independent spirit. It’s walkable, packed with art galleries, quirky boutiques, and some of the best independent restaurants in the city. It’s less about the sprawling mountain views and more about charm, character, and convenience.

Who It’s For

Couples looking for a romantic getaway, art lovers, and anyone who prefers to explore on foot. If your ideal vacation day involves a leisurely morning stroll to a bakery, an afternoon spent browsing art galleries, and an evening enjoying dinner on a patio, OCC is your spot.

Don’t Miss

  • W. Colorado Avenue: This is the main drag. Just park your car and spend a few hours wandering in and out of the shops. Standouts include Eclectic Co. for goods from local artisans and the Michael Garman Museum & Gallery for its fascinating miniature city.
  • Pikes Peak Lemonade: You can’t miss their bright yellow stand. They have a dozen unique, all-natural lemonade flavors. The lavender and spicy pineapple are local favorites. Grab one to sip while you walk.
  • ¡Ay! ¡Ay! ¡Ay! Tacos & Tequila: Forget the fancy places for a moment. This spot, located at 2519 W Colorado Ave, serves up killer street tacos and potent margaritas in a no-frills setting. The carnitas are a must-try.
  • Bancroft Park: The heart of OCC. There are often farmers’ markets on weekends in the summer and beautiful holiday lights in the winter. It’s the perfect place to take a break.

Local’s Tip

While everyone crowds the main street, head one block north or south to Pikes Peak Avenue or Cucharras Street. You’ll find quieter side streets with hidden gem shops and less foot traffic, giving you a more authentic feel for the neighborhood’s historic residential architecture.

The Westside: Your Basecamp for Adventure

The Vibe

When people picture Colorado Springs, they’re usually picturing the Westside. Nestled against the foothills of the Front Range, this area is defined by its immediate access to the outdoors and its jaw-dropping views. It’s more residential and spread out than OCC, with winding roads that lead to trailheads. The air feels a little fresher here. You’re not just looking at the mountains; you’re in them.

Who It’s For

Hikers, mountain bikers, rock climbers, and active families. If your first thought in the morning is “Which trail are we hitting today?” then this is your neighborhood. It’s for travelers who prioritize proximity to natural wonders like Garden of the Gods and Red Rock Canyon Open Space over walkability to shops and restaurants.

Don’t Miss

  • Garden of the Gods: This is non-negotiable, but the key is how you visit. The secret to avoiding the crowds is to go at sunrise or about 90 minutes before sunset. Enter via the main entrance on Gateway Road and do the full loop. The light during these times makes the red rocks glow. For a less-traveled hike, park at the Trading Post and take the Chambers/Bretag/Palmer Trail for a scenic loop with a fraction of the people.
  • Red Rock Canyon Open Space: Locals often prefer this to Garden of the Gods for their daily dose of nature. It offers a similar red rock landscape but with more trails, fewer crowds, and off-leash dog areas. The Quarry Pass Trail gives you a fantastic overview of the park. Find the entrance at 3550 W High St.
  • Manitou Springs: Technically its own town, but it borders the Westside and is a must-visit. This quirky, mountain town has a main street filled with arcades, funky shops, and natural mineral springs you can drink from. It’s also the gateway to the Pikes Peak Cog Railway and the grueling Manitou Incline.
  • Crystal Park Cantina: After a long day of hiking, this is the place to refuel. Located at 178 Crystal Park Rd in Manitou, it has an incredible patio with mountain views. Their margaritas are legendary, and the Cantina Platter gives you a taste of everything.

Local’s Tip

Most visitors just drive the loop at Garden of the Gods. To truly experience it, park your car and explore the Central Garden trails on foot. This is the only way to get up close to the iconic formations like Kissing Camels and Sentinel Spires.

Downtown: For the Urban Explorers and Foodies

The Vibe

Dynamic, revitalized, and buzzing with energy. Downtown Colorado Springs is the cultural and culinary heart of the city. It’s a clean, accessible grid of streets lined with historic buildings that now house modern restaurants, cocktail bars, museums, and independent shops. It’s where the city’s history meets its future. While the mountain views are still present, the focus here is on culture, food, and nightlife.

Who It’s For

Foodies, museum-goers, and anyone who wants to be in the center of the action. If you love trying new restaurants, catching a show, or exploring world-class museums, Downtown is your perfect match. It’s also great for visitors who might not have a car, as much of the core is very walkable.

Don’t Miss

  • U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum: This is an absolute must-do, even if you’re not a huge sports fan. This state-of-the-art, interactive museum is an architectural marvel and tells the powerful stories of Team USA’s athletes. Plan for at least 2-3 hours here. You’ll find it at 200 S Sierra Madre St.
  • COATI Uprise: A modern food hall that captures the new energy of the Springs. It’s a multi-story space with a variety of food vendors (from ramen to burgers to souvlaki), a craft brewery, and a taphouse. It’s the perfect spot for a group that can’t agree on what to eat.
  • The Blue Star: A Downtown institution, this is one of the best fine-dining experiences in the city. The menu is creative, the atmosphere is sophisticated yet relaxed, and the cocktails are top-notch. It’s the perfect place for a special occasion dinner. My go-to is always the Seared Ahi Tuna appetizer.
  • Acacia Park: A lovely urban green space with a playground, fountains in the summer, and an ice rink in the winter. It’s the city’s central gathering spot and hosts events and festivals throughout the year.

Local’s Tip

Look for the public art scattered throughout Downtown. The “Art on the Streets” program means you’ll find sculptures and murals around every corner. Grab a coffee from Pikes Perk and just wander—you’ll be amazed at what you discover.

The Broadmoor Area: For Unforgettable Family Outings

The Vibe

Lush, manicured, and grand. This neighborhood, anchored by the iconic Broadmoor Hotel, feels like a world unto itself. Located in the southwest part of the city, it’s characterized by beautiful, historic homes, tree-lined streets, and a more polished, resort-like atmosphere. It’s nestled right against Cheyenne Canyon, offering a unique combination of refined elegance and rugged nature.

Who It’s For

Families with kids, luxury seekers, and those looking for a quieter, scenic retreat that’s still close to major attractions. If your ideal Colorado Springs travel guide includes world-class zoos and stunning waterfalls, this is your neighborhood.

Don’t Miss

  • Cheyenne Mountain Zoo: Consistently ranked as one of the best zoos in the country, and for good reason. It’s built on the side of a mountain, so be prepared to walk, but the views and animal habitats are incredible. The absolute can’t-miss experience is feeding the giraffes—you get eye-to-eye with these gentle giants. The address is 4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd.
  • Seven Falls: A series of seven cascading waterfalls in a magnificent box canyon. You can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls for an incredible view from the top or take the in-mountain elevator. It’s a breathtaking natural wonder managed by The Broadmoor.
  • North Cheyenne Cañon Park: A city park that feels like a national park. Drive up the scenic North Cheyenne Canyon Road to Helen Hunt Falls (a beautiful, easily accessible waterfall) and continue on to the Starsmore Discovery Center to learn about the area’s geology and wildlife.
  • The Golden Bee: Even if you’re not staying at The Broadmoor, a visit to this authentic 19th-century English pub is a blast. It was actually disassembled panel by panel and shipped from England. Come for the fish and chips and stay for the nightly ragtime piano sing-along. It’s cheesy in the best way possible.

Local’s Tip

Parking at the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo can be a challenge on busy days. Arrive right when they open (or even a few minutes before) to get a spot in the lower lots. If you arrive later, you may have to park further down the mountain and take a shuttle, which can eat into your valuable zoo time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the best area to stay in Colorado Springs for families? A: The Westside and the Broadmoor area are both excellent for families. The Westside offers incredible access to outdoor activities like Garden of the Gods and Red Rock Canyon. The Broadmoor area is home to the world-famous Cheyenne Mountain Zoo and the stunning Seven Falls, providing unforgettable family outings.

Q: Can you get around Colorado Springs without a car? A: It’s challenging. While areas like Downtown and Old Colorado City are very walkable, Colorado Springs is a geographically spread-out city. To access the best hiking trails and major attractions like Pikes Peak and the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, a car is highly recommended.

Q: Where should I stay in Colorado Springs for the best mountain views? A: For the most dramatic, up-close mountain views, staying on the Westside is your best bet. This area is situated right at the base of the foothills, offering panoramic views of Pikes Peak and the surrounding Front Range directly from your Colorado Springs vacation rental.

Q: What is the most walkable neighborhood in Colorado Springs? A: Old Colorado City (OCC) is the most walkable neighborhood for tourists. Its main street, Colorado Avenue, is lined with dozens of shops, art galleries, and restaurants, all easily accessible on foot. Downtown Colorado Springs is also very walkable, with a focus on museums, performing arts, and nightlife.

No matter which neighborhood you choose, you’re in for an incredible trip. The key is to match your home base to your travel style. By picking the right spot, you spend less time driving and more time doing what you came here for: making memories in the shadow of Pikes Peak.
