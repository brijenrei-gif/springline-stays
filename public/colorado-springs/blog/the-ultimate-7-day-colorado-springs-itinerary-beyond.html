The Ultimate 7-Day Colorado Springs Itinerary: Beyond the Postcards

2026-09-23 • 15 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / The Ultimate 7-Day Colorado Springs Itinerary: Beyond the Postcards

So you’ve decided to spend a week in Colorado Springs. Good choice. You’ve probably seen the postcard shots of Garden of the Gods and Pikes Peak, but a truly great trip goes beyond the main attractions. It’s about finding that perfect cup of coffee before a hike, knowing which trail has the best views with the fewest people, and having a home base that feels like a destination in itself.

This isn’t your average list of tourist traps. This is a 7-day plan designed to give you the full Colorado Springs experience—the iconic sights, the local hangouts, and the unforgettable adventures that lie in between. Let’s get started.

Day 1: Conquering the Giants – Pikes Peak & Garden of the Gods

You have to start with the icons. Getting these two titans under your belt on day one gives you context for the entire region’s geography and frees up the rest of your week for deeper exploration.

Morning: Pikes Peak – America’s Mountain

Don’t underestimate this one. Pikes Peak is a half-day adventure, at minimum. You have two main options to reach the 14,115-foot summit:

  1. Drive the Pikes Peak Highway: This 19-mile paved road is breathtaking, but not for the faint of heart. Crucial Tip: You now need a timed entry reservation to drive to the summit between late May and September. Book this weeks in advance. The drive takes about 60-90 minutes each way, not including stops.
  2. Ride The Broadmoor’s Manitou and Pikes Peak Cog Railway: This is the stress-free option. The 9-mile journey offers stunning views without the white-knuckle driving. It’s a 3-hour round trip, including about 40 minutes at the summit. Again, book tickets well in advance, especially in summer.

At the Summit: Grab one of their world-famous high-altitude donuts at the Summit Visitor Center. They’re made with a special recipe that works in the thin air. Walk around, soak in the panoramic views that inspired “America the Beautiful,” and drink plenty of water. Altitude is no joke.

Afternoon: Garden of the Gods

After descending from the peak, grab a quick lunch and head to Garden of the Gods Park (1805 N 30th St). It’s a National Natural Landmark for a reason. The towering red rock fins against the backdrop of Pikes Peak are surreal.

  • First Stop: The Visitor & Nature Center. The exhibits are great, and the terrace has the single best photo-op of the park.
  • Getting Around: You can drive the scenic loop, but the best way to experience the park is on foot. Park at the main lot and walk the Perkins Central Garden Trail. It’s a paved, 1.5-mile loop that takes you right past the most famous formations like Kissing Camels and Sentinel Spires.
  • Local’s Secret: The park gets incredibly crowded by 10 AM. Since you’re going in the afternoon, the crowds might be thinning. An even better strategy is to visit an hour or two before sunset. The way the light hits the red rocks is pure magic, and the parking is much easier to find.

Day 2: A Step Back in Time – Old Colorado City & Manitou Springs

Today is about charm, history, and quirky character. The adjacent towns of Old Colorado City (OCC) and Manitou Springs feel like a world away from the modern city.

Morning: Old Colorado City

Before Colorado Springs was a city, there was Old Colorado City. This historic district along Colorado Avenue is now lined with Victorian buildings housing art galleries, indie boutiques, and fantastic restaurants.

  • Must-Do: Just wander. Pop into the art galleries; they feature a ton of local talent. Check out the boutique shops for unique Colorado souvenirs that aren’t mass-produced.
  • Coffee Break: Stop at 2 South Food + Wine Bar (2 S 25th St) for a great patio vibe or Carnelian Coffee Co. (2623 W Colorado Ave) for a serious cup of coffee.
  • Lunch: Grab a fantastic sandwich at Bon Ton’s Cafe (2601 W Colorado Ave), a local institution since the 1880s.

Afternoon: Manitou Springs

Just a five-minute drive west of OCC, Manitou Springs is a funky, bohemian mountain town nestled at the base of Pikes Peak.

  • The Vibe: It’s all about the water. The town is famous for its 8 naturally carbonated mineral springs. Pick up a map and a tasting cup from the Visitor Center and tour the different springs—each has a unique taste and mineral content.
  • The Manitou Incline: If you’re an extreme fitness enthusiast, you can’t miss this. It’s a mile of stairs gaining 2,000 feet of elevation. It’s a beast. Reservations are required. For everyone else, watching people attempt it is entertainment enough.
  • The Penny Arcade: A must-visit for all ages. It’s a sprawling, old-school arcade with hundreds of games, from vintage pinball and skeeball to classic video games. Bring a roll of quarters (they have change machines) and lose track of time.

Day 3: Wild Encounters & Canyon Vistas

Get ready for some elevation and amazing animal encounters. Today, we’re heading to the Cheyenne Mountain area.

Morning: Cheyenne Mountain Zoo

This is consistently ranked one of the best zoos in the country, and for good reason. The Cheyenne Mountain Zoo (4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd) is America’s only mountain zoo.

  • Pro Tip: Wear comfortable shoes. The exhibits are built into the mountainside, so you’ll be walking uphill.
  • Don’t Miss: The giraffe herd. You can buy some lettuce and feed them by hand as they tower over you from their elevated boardwalk. It’s an unforgettable experience. The Mountaineer Sky Ride also offers incredible views of the zoo and the city below.
  • Timing: Get there right when they open to see the animals at their most active and to beat the biggest crowds.

Afternoon: North Cheyenne Cañon Park

Just down the road from the zoo is this stunning city park that feels like a national park. The drive up North Cheyenne Canyon Road is gorgeous, winding alongside the creek.

  • Helen Hunt Falls: An easy-to-access, beautiful waterfall right off the road. You can take a short, steep hike to the top for a different perspective.
  • Starsmore Discovery Center: A great place to start at the park entrance to grab a map and learn about the canyon’s geology and history.
  • Hiking: For a moderate hike with a huge payoff, drive further up the road to the Starsmore/Seven Bridges Trailhead. It’s a popular but beautiful trek that crisscrosses the creek over a series of wooden bridges.

Day 4: Rest, Recharge, & Explore Like a Local

You’ve had three packed days. It’s time for a “choose your own adventure” day at a slower pace. This is your chance to live like a local.

Option 1: The Museum Buff

  • U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum: This modern, interactive museum is a must-see. It’s incredibly well-done and inspiring for all ages. Plan for at least 2-3 hours.
  • Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum: Housed in the beautiful 1903 El Paso County Courthouse, this free museum offers a fascinating look at the region’s history, from its Native American roots to its founding as a resort town.

Option 2: The Urban Explorer

  • Downtown Colorado Springs: Explore the shops and restaurants along Tejon Street. Grab lunch at a spot like Odyssey Gastropub (311 N Tejon St) for creative comfort food.
  • The Broadmoor Neighborhood: Even if you’re not staying there, driving or walking through the neighborhood surrounding The Broadmoor hotel is a treat, with its stunning, historic mansions and manicured lawns.
  • Memorial Park: Walk the loop around Prospect Lake with fantastic views of Pikes Peak. It’s a flat, easy walk and a local favorite.

Option 3: The Coffee & Chill Seeker

  • Start with Coffee: Hit up The Wild Goose Meeting House (401 N Tejon St) for a great atmosphere and excellent coffee.
  • Browse a Bookstore: Spend an hour getting lost in the stacks at Poor Richard’s Bookstore, which is part of a complex that includes a restaurant, toy store, and coffee shop.
  • Catch a Movie: Visit the quirky Kimball’s Peak Three Theater (115 E Pikes Peak Ave), a downtown indie cinema.

Day 5: The Royal Treatment – The Broadmoor & Seven Falls

Today is about indulging in the more luxurious side of Colorado Springs. The Broadmoor is a world-class resort that has defined elegance in this city for over a century.

Morning: The Broadmoor

You don’t have to be a guest to experience the grandeur of The Broadmoor (1 Lake Ave). Park in their garage (there’s a fee) and give yourself a couple of hours to explore.

  • Walk the Grounds: Stroll around Cheyenne Lake, admire the perfectly manicured gardens, and walk through the historic hotel lobbies. The art and architecture are incredible.
  • Coffee or a Drink: Grab a coffee at Espresso’s or, for a real treat, have a classic cocktail at The Hotel Bar. It’s pricey, but the ambiance is worth it.

Afternoon: Seven Falls

Owned by The Broadmoor, Seven Falls is a series of seven cascading waterfalls in a magnificent 1,250-foot-wall box canyon.

  • Getting There: You must park at The Broadmoor and take a complimentary shuttle to the entrance. You cannot drive directly to the falls.
  • The Experience: Once there, you can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls to the top, where you’ll find hiking trails and spectacular views. If the stairs aren’t for you, there’s an in-mountain elevator that takes you to the “Eagle’s Nest” viewing platform.
  • Restaurant 1858: Tucked at the base of the falls, this is one of the most unique dining settings you’ll ever find. It specializes in Colorado cuisine. Reservations are highly recommended.

Day 6: Red Rocks & Rocky Mountain Brews

Let’s get back on the trails. Red Rock Canyon Open Space is often called the “local’s Garden of the Gods.” It offers similar stunning rock formations but with more trails and fewer crowds.

Morning: Red Rock Canyon Open Space

Located just off Highway 24 (3550 W High St), this park is a playground for hikers, rock climbers, and dog owners.

  • The Trails: The trail network is extensive. A great introductory loop is the Contemplative Trail to the Red Rock Canyon Trail. It gives you a great mix of views without being too strenuous.
  • Dog-Friendly Fun: The park has designated off-leash dog loops, making it a paradise for four-legged friends.
  • Local’s Tip: The main parking lots fill up, but there are several lots along the road. The one furthest west often has more space and provides access to different, quieter trails.

Afternoon: Westside Breweries

The craft beer scene in Colorado Springs is phenomenal. After your hike, you’ve earned a cold one. The west side has some fantastic options.

  • Fossil Craft Beer Company (2845 Ore Mill Rd #1): Known for their quality brews and friendly, neighborhood vibe.
  • Red Leg Brewing Company (2323 Garden of the Gods Rd): A veteran-owned brewery with a massive, beautiful facility featuring a huge rooftop patio with some of the best Pikes Peak views in the city. They always have a variety of food trucks on site.

Day 7: Farewell Views & Brunch

It’s your last day. Time for one more dose of Colorado beauty and a fantastic meal before you head home.

Morning: Palmer Park

This is another local gem. Palmer Park (3650 Maizeland Rd) is a geological anomaly—a mesa of unique rock formations right in the middle of the city.

  • The Drive: You can drive the scenic loop to the top for panoramic views of the entire Pikes Peak region.
  • The Hike: There are tons of short trails that wind through the rock formations. The Templeton Trail is a great option for a relatively easy walk with big rewards.

Late Morning: The Farewell Brunch

End your trip on a high note with a delicious brunch.

  • Urban Egg: With several locations, they are a powerhouse of breakfast food. The creative benedicts and pancake flights are legendary. Expect a wait on weekends.
  • Shuga’s (702 S Cascade Ave): Known for its quirky, vibrant atmosphere and incredible food. The “Shuga’s Favorite” sandwich and their spicy shrimp soup are absolute must-tries, even for brunch.

Colorado Springs Trip Planning FAQ

How many days do you really need in Colorado Springs? While you can see a few highlights in a long weekend, a 5 to 7-day trip is ideal. This allows you to explore major attractions like Pikes Peak and Garden of the Gods without rushing, and also gives you time to discover local favorites, enjoy some hikes, and have a rest day.

What is the best month to visit Colorado Springs? June through September offers the warmest weather and ensures all attractions, like the Pikes Peak Highway, are fully open. However, September is arguably the sweet spot—the weather is still beautiful, the summer crowds have thinned, and the aspen trees in the mountains begin to turn a brilliant gold.

Is it better to stay in Colorado Springs or Manitou Springs? It depends on your travel style. Colorado Springs is more spread out and offers a wider variety of dining, shopping, and lodging, making it a great central base for exploring the entire region. Manitou Springs is a smaller, quainter, and more walkable town with a bohemian vibe, but it can get very crowded and parking is difficult.

Do I need to worry about altitude sickness in Colorado Springs? Yes. The city sits at an elevation of 6,035 feet. Most visitors experience only mild symptoms, if any (like a slight headache or shortness of breath). The key is to acclimate. Take it easy on your first day, avoid strenuous activity, and drink much more water than you normally would. Be especially mindful if you’re heading up Pikes Peak (14,115 feet).

A week in Colorado Springs offers an incredible mix of natural wonder, historic charm, and modern adventure. From the summit of Pikes Peak to the floor of a red rock canyon, you’re in for an unforgettable trip.
