Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Local's Guide to the Best Neighborhoods

2026-10-07 • 12 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Local's Guide to the Best Neighborhoods

The biggest mistake visitors make in Colorado Springs isn’t what they choose to see—it’s where they choose to stay. The city is wonderfully spread out, nestled against the foothills of the Rockies, and your home base can mean the difference between spending your vacation in traffic or spending it on a trail. Picking the right neighborhood doesn’t just give you a place to sleep; it defines the entire vibe of your trip.

Are you looking for a walkable historic district with art galleries and cozy cafes? Or would you prefer waking up to panoramic mountain views from a quiet, secluded hillside? This isn’t just a travel guide; it’s the key to unlocking the perfect Colorado Springs experience by matching your travel style to the right slice of the city.

Old Colorado City & The Westside: For History Buffs and Walkable Charm

Before Colorado Springs was Colorado Springs, there was Old Colorado City. This was the original capital of the Colorado Territory, and that Wild West spirit is still palpable today. Staying here means you’re trading sprawling suburbs for narrow streets lined with 19th-century brick buildings housing independent art galleries, quirky boutiques, and some of the city’s best local restaurants.

The Vibe: Historic, artistic, and incredibly walkable. You can spend a full day just meandering along West Colorado Avenue, popping into shops and art co-ops. The evenings are buzzy but not overwhelming, with patios full of people enjoying local brews and fantastic Italian food. It’s the perfect spot if your ideal vacation involves parking the car and exploring on foot.

What’s Nearby?

This neighborhood is the undisputed champion of proximity to iconic natural landmarks. You are literally minutes away from: * Garden of the Gods: A 5-minute drive gets you to the park entrance. Pro tip: Go early in the morning (before 8 AM) to see the rising sun light up the red rocks and to grab a parking spot at the popular Perkins Central Garden Trailhead. * Red Rock Canyon Open Space: Just south of Highway 24, this is the local’s alternative to Garden of the Gods. It has similar stunning rock formations but with fewer crowds and more dog-friendly trails. * Ghost Town Museum: A fun, kitschy look at the region’s past, located at 2501 W Colorado Ave.

Local Favorites:

  • Coffee: Jives Coffee Lounge at 16 Colbrunn Ct is a hidden gem tucked away in a courtyard. Great coffee and a quiet place to plan your day.
  • Dinner: You can’t go wrong with Paravicini’s Italian Bistro. It’s loud, it’s classic, and the Veal Saltimbocca is legendary. Reservations are a must.
  • Drinks: Mother Muffs is a neighborhood institution. It’s quirky, cozy, and perfect for a post-hike beer.

The Broadmoor Area: For Luxury Seekers and Mountain Seclusion

Nestled in the southwest corner of the city, the Broadmoor area is defined by the grand, historic hotel of the same name. The neighborhood surrounding it is one of the most beautiful and affluent in Colorado Springs. Think winding roads, towering pine trees, stunning architecture, and the occasional deer wandering across a perfectly manicured lawn.

The Vibe: Upscale, serene, and deeply connected to nature. This isn’t a walkable “downtown” area; it’s a place you retreat to. The appeal is feeling like you’re tucked away in a mountain enclave while still being a 10-minute drive from the city’s core. It’s ideal for multi-generational family trips, romantic getaways, or anyone looking for a more resort-like, amenity-rich vacation.

What’s Nearby?

The Broadmoor area is a hub for some of the city’s most polished attractions: * Cheyenne Mountain Zoo: Consistently ranked one of the best zoos in the country. The elevation is no joke, so be prepared for a workout, but feeding the giraffes with the city sprawling below is an unforgettable experience. Located at 4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd. * Seven Falls: A series of seven cascading waterfalls in a 1,250-foot-wall box canyon. You can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls for incredible views. It’s owned by The Broadmoor, so expect a pristine (and ticketed) experience. * North Cheyenne Cañon Park: For a free, more rugged alternative to Seven Falls, this city park offers miles of hiking trails, beautiful Helen Hunt Falls, and scenic drives up Gold Camp Road.

Downtown Colorado Springs: For Urban Explorers and Foodies

If your vacation priorities include a top-tier food scene, craft cocktails, museums, and a central location for day trips, Downtown Colorado Springs is your spot. In recent years, the downtown core has exploded with energy. Tejon Street is the main artery, lined with fantastic restaurants, bars, and local shops.

The Vibe: Energetic, convenient, and surprisingly scenic. Unlike many city centers, Downtown Colorado Springs always has Pikes Peak looming in the background, a constant reminder of the wilderness just a short drive away. It’s the best of both worlds: urban amenities with unparalleled access to the outdoors.

What’s Nearby?

Everything is at your fingertips when you stay downtown. * U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum: An absolute must-see. This modern, interactive museum is an incredible tribute to Team USA athletes. * Pikes Peak Center for the Performing Arts: Check the schedule for concerts, Broadway shows, and performances by the Colorado Springs Philharmonic. * Acacia Park: A lovely green space in the heart of downtown, often hosting farmer’s markets and events.

Local Favorites:

  • Brunch: Urban Egg is a local chain for a reason. Their pancake flights are legendary. Get there early or join the waitlist online.
  • Dinner: For a special occasion, The Rabbit Hole is an underground (literally) restaurant with a creative, New American menu and an “Alice in Wonderland” theme. For something more casual, Shuga’s has an eclectic menu, amazing cocktails (try the “Spicy Brazilian Coconut Shrimp Soup”), and a fantastic patio.
  • Drinks: The Archives is a moody, speakeasy-style bar tucked away underground. It’s all about classic, expertly crafted cocktails.

Manitou Springs: For Quirky Vibes and Pikes Peak Access

Technically its own city, Manitou Springs is tucked into a canyon at the base of Pikes Peak, just west of Colorado Springs. Staying here feels like stepping into a different world. The vibe is decidedly bohemian, a little eccentric, and fiercely independent. The main street is a colorful collection of art galleries, crystal shops, old-school penny arcades, and restaurants.

The Vibe: Funky, free-spirited, and tourist-focused. It’s a place of healing mineral springs (you can taste them from public fountains), challenging hikes, and pure fun. Be aware that it gets very crowded, especially in the summer, and parking can be a serious challenge. It’s best for travelers who love being in the middle of a bustling, unique atmosphere.

What’s Nearby?

Manitou is the gateway to some of the region’s most famous challenges: * Manitou Incline: The holy grail for fitness enthusiasts. This trail gains nearly 2,000 feet of elevation in less than a mile up the remnants of a former cog railway. It’s brutally difficult, and reservations are now required. * Pikes Peak Cog Railway: The newly renovated railway takes you on a stunning journey from the Manitou depot all the way to the 14,115-foot summit of Pikes Peak. Book tickets well in advance. * Cave of the Winds Mountain Park: Offers guided cave tours and thrilling attractions like a zip line and a canyon swing.

Rockrimmon & The Northwest: For Unbeatable Views and Trail Access

For those who want to wake up, look out the window, and be immediately humbled by a massive mountain view, the northwest part of the city is the answer. Neighborhoods like Rockrimmon are built into the bluffs and hills, offering breathtaking, panoramic views of Pikes Peak and the entire Front Range.

The Vibe: Quiet, residential, and outdoors-focused. This part of town is less about walkable entertainment and more about having incredible nature right outside your door. It’s perfect for families or groups who want space to spread out and prioritize hiking and scenic drives over nightlife. The trade-off for the views is that you’ll be driving 15-20 minutes to get to most major restaurants and attractions.

What’s Nearby?

  • Ute Valley Park: A local favorite for hiking and mountain biking with a fantastic network of trails and dramatic sandstone cliffs.
  • Garden of the Gods: You can easily access the park from the north, often avoiding the main traffic coming from Highway 24.
  • United States Air Force Academy: The visitor center is open to the public, and the iconic Cadet Chapel (when not under renovation) is a marvel of modern architecture. The drive through the campus is scenic and worthwhile.

Frequently Asked Questions About Where to Stay in Colorado Springs

What is the best area to stay in Colorado Springs for families?

For families with younger kids, the Broadmoor area is fantastic due to its proximity to the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo and Seven Falls. For families with older kids or those who want more space and amenities like a game room or hot tub, the Northwest/Rockrimmon area offers incredible views and larger homes, like the Stunning Mountain Views, Hot Tub, Game Room Springline Stays property. Old Colorado City is also a great option for its walkability and proximity to Garden of the Gods.

Is it better to stay in Downtown Colorado Springs or Old Colorado City?

It depends on your preference. Choose Downtown if you want a more urban feel with a wider variety of modern restaurants, bars, and museums. Choose Old Colorado City if you prefer a quaint, historic atmosphere with art galleries, boutiques, and a more relaxed, small-town vibe. They are only a 10-minute drive from each other, so you can easily enjoy both regardless of where you stay.

Can you visit Colorado Springs without a car?

While you could technically manage a short trip staying in Downtown or Old Colorado City and using ride-sharing services, it is highly discouraged. The best things to do in Colorado Springs, from Pikes Peak to the myriad of hiking trails, are spread out and require a vehicle to access efficiently. Renting a car will give you the freedom to explore the entire Pikes Peak region.

How many days do you need to see Colorado Springs?

To get a good feel for the city and see the main attractions without rushing, plan for at least 3 to 4 full days. This gives you time for Pikes Peak, Garden of the Gods, a hike or two, and exploring one or two neighborhoods. If you want to add a day trip to the Royal Gorge Bridge or explore more deeply, a 5 to 7-day trip is ideal.
