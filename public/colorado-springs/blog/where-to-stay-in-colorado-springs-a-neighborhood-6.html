Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Neighborhood Guide for Every Traveler

2026-09-26 • 12 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Neighborhood Guide for Every Traveler

Choosing the right neighborhood in Colorado Springs can make or break your trip. This isn’t a compact, walkable city where everything is just around the corner. Your home base determines whether your vacation is filled with scenic mountain drives or frustrating traffic on I-25. It’s the difference between a 5-minute hop to a trailhead and a 45-minute trek across town.

Think of this as your insider’s map to the city’s distinct personalities. We’ll break down the vibe of each key area, who it’s perfect for, and what you can’t miss nearby. Find your perfect launchpad, and the rest of your Rocky Mountain adventure will fall into place.

Old Colorado City: For History Buffs and Boutique Shoppers

The Vibe

Step back in time. Old Colorado City (OCC) was the original capital of the Colorado Territory, and it still holds onto its Wild West charm with beautifully preserved brick buildings, gas-lamp-lined streets, and a fiercely independent spirit. It’s incredibly walkable, packed with art galleries, quirky boutiques, and some of the city’s best restaurants. The energy here is relaxed, historic, and perfect for strolling.

Perfect For

Couples on a romantic getaway, history enthusiasts, and anyone who loves to park their car for the evening and explore on foot. If your ideal vacation day involves a morning coffee, gallery hopping, and a fantastic dinner without getting back behind the wheel, OCC is your spot.

What’s Nearby

  • Colorado Avenue: This is the main drag. Spend hours popping into art galleries, Southwestern jewelry shops, and the year-round Christmas store, “Eve’s Revolution.”
  • Pizzeria Rustica: An absolute must-eat. Authentic Neapolitan pizza cooked in a wood-fired oven. The Diavola is a personal favorite if you like a little heat. Find it at 2527 W Colorado Ave.
  • Red Rock Canyon Open Space: Just a 5-minute drive away, this is a local’s alternative to Garden of the Gods. It offers similar stunning red rock formations but with fewer crowds and more dog-friendly trails.
  • Garden of the Gods: The main entrance is less than a 10-minute drive, making it easy to pop over for a sunrise or sunset stroll.

The Broadmoor Area: For Luxury Seekers and Mountain Scenery

The Vibe

Elegant, pristine, and nestled right against the mountains. This neighborhood is defined by the world-famous Broadmoor Hotel. The surrounding area is characterized by stately homes, manicured lawns, and an air of quiet sophistication. The views are jaw-dropping, with Cheyenne Mountain looming dramatically overhead.

Perfect For

Travelers celebrating a special occasion, golf enthusiasts, and anyone looking for a peaceful, scenic retreat that feels a world away from the city bustle (while still being only 15 minutes from downtown).

What’s Nearby

  • The Broadmoor Hotel: You don’t have to be a guest to enjoy its splendor. Walk around Cheyenne Lake, grab a cocktail at the Hotel Bar, or book a spa treatment.
  • Seven Falls: A series of seven cascading waterfalls in a magnificent 1,250-foot-wall box canyon. You can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls for an incredible view from the top.
  • Cheyenne Mountain Zoo: Consistently ranked one of the best zoos in the country, this is America’s only mountain zoo. Hand-feed the famous giraffe herd with the city sprawling below you—it’s an unforgettable experience. The address is 4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd.
  • North Cheyenne Cañon Park: Drive up the scenic byway to Helen Hunt Falls or hike deeper into the canyon on trails like the Columbine Trail. It’s a stunning, free city park.

Westside & Manitou-Adjacent: For Unbeatable Access to Nature

The Vibe

Adventurous, a bit quirky, and all about the outdoors. This area, located between Old Colorado City and the eccentric town of Manitou Springs, is the undisputed epicenter for hiking and natural attractions. It’s less about polished storefronts and more about well-worn hiking boots and cars with bike racks. The focus here is getting out the door and onto the trail as quickly as possible.

Perfect For

Hikers, rock climbers, trail runners, and any active group that prioritizes proximity to adventure over city amenities. If your Colorado Springs travel guide is mostly a list of trails, this is your neighborhood.

What’s Nearby

  • Garden of the Gods: You’ll be minutes from multiple entrances to this National Natural Landmark. The secret to avoiding crowds? Go early on a weekday through the 30th Street entrance and head straight for the Palmer Trail for a loop around the entire park.
  • Manitou Springs: A funky, artsy mountain town with a main street full of arcades, unique shops, and natural mineral springs you can drink from. It’s the starting point for the grueling but rewarding Manitou Incline.
  • Pikes Peak Highway: The entrance to the scenic drive (or cog railway) up America’s Mountain is right here.
  • Cave of the Winds Mountain Park: Explore stunning cave systems on a guided tour or get your adrenaline pumping on their outdoor thrill rides.

Downtown Colorado Springs: For Urban Explorers and Foodies

The Vibe

Energetic, modern, and increasingly vibrant. Downtown Colorado Springs has undergone a major revitalization. It’s the cultural and culinary heart of the city, with a growing number of top-tier restaurants, craft cocktail bars, museums, and public art installations. It’s a grid of tree-lined streets that feels both historic and forward-looking.

Perfect For

Foodies, museum lovers, and travelers who want to be in the middle of the action. If you’re looking for nightlife or want to explore the city’s cultural scene, staying downtown is your best bet.

What’s Nearby

  • U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum: A stunning, state-of-the-art interactive museum that is a must-see for everyone, not just sports fans.
  • Acacia Park: A central green space that often hosts farmers’ markets and events.
  • Tejon Street: This is the main north-south artery for dining and drinking. Don’t miss local favorites like Shuga’s for its cozy, eclectic vibe and famous “Cafe Shuga,” or Odyssey Gastropub for elevated pub fare.
  • Pikes Peak Center for the Performing Arts: Catch a show from the Colorado Springs Philharmonic or a touring Broadway production.

Rockrimmon: For Central Access and Bluffs Views

The Vibe

Quietly residential with spectacular “in-the-city” nature. Nestled in the northwestern part of town, Rockrimmon is known for its dramatic bluffs, winding roads, and incredible views. It feels removed from the tourist hubs but offers fantastic centrality, sitting right off I-25. You can get almost anywhere in the city—north or south—in about 15-20 minutes.

Perfect For

Travelers who want a peaceful, scenic home base with easy highway access to explore the entire Pikes Peak region. It’s great for those who plan to visit attractions both north (like the Air Force Academy) and south (like the Zoo).

What’s Nearby

  • Ute Valley Park: A local gem for hiking and mountain biking. The trails wind through rocky ridges and meadows, offering great views of Pikes Peak and the Front Range.
  • Garden of the Gods: Easily accessible via the less-crowded northern entrance on 30th Street.
  • Pro Rodeo Hall of Fame: Discover the history of America’s original sport.
  • Quick I-25 Access: This is the real perk. You can be on your way to Denver, Castle Rock, or anywhere in Colorado Springs in minutes.

Northgate: For Families and Air Force Academy Visitors

The Vibe

Modern, suburban, and growing fast. The Northgate area, on the far north side of Colorado Springs, is defined by newer housing developments, wide-open spaces, and its proximity to the United States Air Force Academy (USAFA). It’s very family-friendly, with lots of parks and modern conveniences.

Perfect For

Families visiting cadets at the USAFA, groups attending events at the Air Force stadium, and travelers who prefer newer, larger homes and don’t mind being a bit further from the main tourist attractions like Garden of the Gods.

What’s Nearby

  • United States Air Force Academy: The Visitor Center is open to the public and offers incredible views of the iconic Cadet Chapel (currently under renovation but still visible). You can also walk trails on the grounds and visit the Honor Court.
  • Scheels and Bass Pro Shops: These are more than just stores; they’re massive retail destinations with indoor Ferris wheels, aquariums, and restaurants. Great for a rainy day with the kids.
  • Topgolf: A popular entertainment venue for all ages.
  • Easy access to Monument and Palmer Lake: Explore these charming small towns just a few minutes further north.

FAQ: Choosing Your Colorado Springs Home Base

What is the best area to stay in Colorado Springs for families?

For families with younger kids, the Northgate or Rockrimmon areas are excellent choices due to the prevalence of larger, newer homes with more space and amenities like yards and game rooms. For families focused on outdoor adventures, the Westside offers unbeatable proximity to parks and trails, minimizing drive time with antsy kids.

Is it better to stay in Colorado Springs or Manitou Springs?

It depends on your preference. Stay in Manitou Springs if you want a quirky, walkable, small-mountain-town experience and plan to focus your activities around the Incline and Pikes Peak. Stay in Colorado Springs (specifically the Westside or Old Colorado City) if you want easier access to a wider variety of restaurants, shops, and attractions across the entire Pikes Peak region. Manitou can feel a bit isolated and has notoriously tricky parking.

Do I need a car in Colorado Springs?

Yes, absolutely. Colorado Springs is very spread out, and public transportation is limited for tourist purposes. To efficiently get between major attractions like Garden of the Gods, Pikes Peak, and the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, a car is essential. The only exception is if you stay in Old Colorado City or Downtown and plan to spend your entire trip exploring only that immediate area on foot.

Where is the most walkable neighborhood in Colorado Springs?

Old Colorado City is by far the most walkable neighborhood for tourists. You can easily spend a full day or two exploring its main street, side streets, restaurants, and shops without needing a car. Downtown Colorado Springs is a close second, with a great concentration of museums, parks, and dining options within a few blocks.
