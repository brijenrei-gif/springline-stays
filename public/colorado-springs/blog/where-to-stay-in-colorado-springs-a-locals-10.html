Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Local's Guide to the Best Neighborhoods

2026-10-14 • 12 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / Where to Stay in Colorado Springs: A Local's Guide to the Best Neighborhoods

Choosing the right neighborhood in Colorado Springs is the single most important decision you’ll make for your trip. This isn’t just about finding a place to sleep; it’s about curating your entire experience. Do you want to walk to historic art galleries and cafes, or do you want a quiet basecamp with epic mountain views and a hot tub? Your home base determines your daily rhythm, your commute to the trails, and your evening vibes.

Forget the generic hotel zones. Let’s break down the real character of Colorado Springs’ best neighborhoods so you can find the perfect launchpad for your adventure.

Old Colorado City: For History Buffs & Boutique Lovers

If you want to feel like you’ve stepped back into the Wild West—but with excellent espresso and art galleries—Old Colorado City (OCC) is your spot. This National Historic District was the original capital of the Colorado Territory, and that frontier spirit is still alive in its beautifully preserved brick buildings and tree-lined streets.

The Vibe: Charming, walkable, and buzzing with independent spirit. It’s less about chain stores and more about one-of-a-kind boutiques, art galleries, and fantastic local restaurants. It’s the perfect place to spend an afternoon wandering without a plan.

What’s Nearby?

This is arguably the best location for hitting the top attractions. You are literally minutes away from: * Garden of the Gods: A 5-10 minute drive. You can beat the crowds by getting there at sunrise. * Red Rock Canyon Open Space: A 5-minute drive. This is the local’s alternative to Garden of the Gods, with fewer crowds and more dog-friendly trails. * Old Colorado City’s Main Strip (Colorado Ave): You can walk to dozens of shops and restaurants.

Local Favorites in OCC

  • Dining: You can’t go wrong with Paravicini’s Italian Bistro (2802 W Colorado Ave), but the real local secret is to get a reservation well in advance or try for a late lunch on a weekday. For something more casual, the Neapolitan-style pies at Pizzeria Rustica (2527 W Colorado Ave) are legendary.
  • Coffee: Carnelian Coffee Co. (2629 W Colorado Ave) is the go-to for a perfect latte and a cozy spot to plan your day.
  • Shopping: Skip the typical souvenir shops and check out Eclectic Co. (2518 W Colorado Ave), a fantastic co-op featuring jewelry, clothing, and art from local Colorado makers.

For a group or family that wants to be in the heart of the action, a home base right here is ideal. You get the neighborhood feel with unbeatable access to the parks. After a long day of hiking in Red Rock Canyon, you can come back and gather around the fire pit with epic views of the Front Range. Plus, being pet-friendly is a huge win in this outdoor-loving town.

Best for: First-time visitors, couples, and small groups who want walkability and proximity to the most famous natural landmarks.

Manitou Springs: For the Quirky, Artsy & Active

Nestled at the base of Pikes Peak, Manitou Springs is Colorado Springs’ eccentric little sister. It’s a town that marches to the beat of its own drum. With a vibrant arts scene, historic penny arcade, and naturally carbonated mineral springs you can drink from, Manitou is an experience in itself.

The Vibe: Bohemian, eclectic, and a little bit magical. The streets are narrow, the buildings are colorful, and there’s a tangible creative energy in the air. It’s a haven for artists, hikers, and anyone looking for something a little different.

What’s Nearby?

Manitou is the gateway to some of the area’s most challenging and rewarding adventures: * Manitou Incline: The legendary staircase of pain and glory. If you’re going to attempt it, staying in Manitou makes a 5 AM start time much more manageable. * Pikes Peak Cog Railway: The train to the summit of “America’s Mountain” leaves directly from Manitou. * Cave of the Winds Mountain Park: Just up the road, offering cave tours and thrill rides.

Local Favorites in Manitou Springs

  • The Mineral Springs: The whole town was built around these. Grab a map and a cup and do a walking tour to sample all eight of them. They each have a slightly different taste and mineral content. The one at Wheeler Town Clock is a local favorite.
  • The Penny Arcade: Don’t let the name fool you. This sprawling, multi-building arcade has everything from hundred-year-old horse racing games to modern video games. It’s a blast for all ages and a great way to spend an afternoon. Find it at 930 Manitou Ave.
  • Dining: For a classic Manitou experience, grab a beer and a burger at The Keg Lounge (730 Manitou Ave). For something a bit more refined, The Cliff House at Pikes Peak (306 Cañon Ave) offers an incredible fine dining experience.

Best for: Adventure seekers, artists, and travelers who prefer a funky, walkable town over a city feel. Note: Parking can be very challenging here, especially in the summer. Staying in a walkable rental is a huge advantage.

Downtown Colorado Springs: For Foodies & Urban Explorers

If your ideal vacation involves sampling craft cocktails, visiting world-class museums, and having a dozen top-tier restaurants within walking distance, Downtown Colorado Springs is your spot. The city has invested heavily in revitalizing its urban core, and it shows. It’s clean, vibrant, and packed with things to do.

The Vibe: Energetic, modern, and increasingly sophisticated. It’s the cultural and culinary heart of the city, where historic architecture meets modern restaurants and public art installations.

What’s Nearby?

You’re in the center of the action: * U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum: A stunning, state-of-the-art tribute to Team USA. It’s a must-see. * Pikes Peak Center for the Performing Arts: Hosts everything from the Colorado Springs Philharmonic to touring Broadway shows. * Acacia Park: A central green space that hosts farmers’ markets in the summer and an ice rink in the winter.

Local Favorites Downtown

  • Unique Dining: You have to try The Rabbit Hole (101 N Tejon St), a whimsical, underground New American restaurant with an Alice in Wonderland theme. Finding the entrance in the sidewalk is part of the fun.
  • Rooftop Bars: Lumen8 Rooftop Social (402 S Tejon St) offers incredible cocktails with panoramic views of Pikes Peak and the city. Go for sunset.
  • Breakfast: Urban Egg (28 S Tejon St) is a powerhouse of a breakfast spot. The pancake flights are famous for a reason. Get there early or join the waitlist online.

Best for: Foodies, couples on a romantic getaway, and anyone who wants a more urban vacation experience with easy access to culture and nightlife.

The Broadmoor Area: For Scenery, Luxury & Seclusion

Circling the historic, world-renowned Broadmoor Hotel, this neighborhood is synonymous with elegance and natural beauty. Tucked against the mountains in the southwest part of the city, the area is characterized by stunning homes, manicured landscapes, and a quieter, more residential feel.

The Vibe: Upscale, serene, and scenic. The roads wind through the forested foothills, offering spectacular views. It feels a world away from the city hustle, yet you’re only a 10-15 minute drive from downtown.

What’s Nearby?

This is your base for some of the most spectacular attractions: * Cheyenne Mountain Zoo: Consistently ranked one of the best zoos in the country. The giraffe feeding experience, with the city spread out below you, is unforgettable. * Seven Falls: A series of seven cascading waterfalls in a magnificent box canyon. You can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls for an incredible view. * North Cheyenne Cañon Park: A city park that feels like a national park, with miles of hiking trails, picnic areas, and the beautiful Helen Hunt Falls.

Local Favorites in the Broadmoor Area

  • Walk the Broadmoor Grounds: Even if you aren’t a guest, you can explore the stunning property. Stroll around Cheyenne Lake, admire the architecture, and grab a classic cocktail at the Hotel Bar. It’s a timeless Colorado Springs experience.
  • Golden Bee: For a more lively evening, head to this authentic 19th-century English pub (it was disassembled and shipped from England!) at The Broadmoor. Get ready for the nightly ragtime piano sing-along.
  • Sacred Ground Cafe (1801 W Cheyenne Rd): A cozy, independent coffee shop and meeting spot loved by locals in the area. A great place to fuel up before heading to the zoo.

Best for: Travelers seeking a quiet and scenic retreat, luxury enthusiasts, and families who plan to spend a lot of time at the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo and Seven Falls.

Northgate & North Colorado Springs: For Families & Extended Stays

The northern part of Colorado Springs, particularly the Northgate area, is one of the city’s fastest-growing regions. It’s characterized by newer homes, wide-open spaces, and fantastic views of the Air Force Academy and the Rampart Range.

The Vibe: Suburban, family-friendly, and convenient. This area is all about easy living, with modern amenities, great shopping centers, and plenty of space. It’s the practical choice for large groups or families who want a comfortable home base with all the creature comforts.

What’s Nearby?

  • U.S. Air Force Academy: The visitor center and the iconic Cadet Chapel are must-sees.
  • Garden of the Gods: While not as close as OCC, it’s still an easy 15-20 minute drive down I-25.
  • Great Wolf Lodge: A massive indoor water park and resort that’s a huge draw for families with kids.

Local Favorites in North Colorado Springs

  • Scheels: This isn’t just a sporting goods store; it’s an attraction. It has a 65-foot indoor Ferris wheel, a 16,000-gallon aquarium, and interactive games. It’s a great rainy-day activity. Find it at 1226 Interquest Pkwy.
  • Bass Pro Shop: Another retail giant that doubles as an experience, with huge fish tanks and impressive wildlife displays.
  • Dining: The Northgate and Interquest areas are packed with reliable, family-friendly chains and a growing number of excellent independent spots. ViewHouse (7114 Campus Dr) has a huge rooftop patio with great food and even better views.

Colorado Springs Neighborhood FAQ

What is the best area to stay in Colorado Springs for families? For families with younger children, the Northgate area is fantastic due to its modern, spacious homes with amenities like game rooms and yards. The Broadmoor area is another top choice for its proximity to the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo. For families with older kids who appreciate walkability, Old Colorado City is a great option.

Is it better to stay in Colorado Springs or Manitou Springs? This depends entirely on your travel style. If you want a quirky, bohemian town with a focus on hiking (especially the Incline) and a very walkable, unique downtown, choose Manitou Springs. If you prefer more variety in dining and easier access to the broader Pikes Peak region, including Garden of the Gods and Downtown, a central neighborhood in Colorado Springs like Old Colorado City is more practical.

What part of Colorado Springs is closest to Pikes Peak? Manitou Springs is the closest town to the Pikes Peak Cog Railway and the Pikes Peak Highway entrance. Cascade, a small community just west of Manitou, is also right at the base. However, the mountain is so massive that it dominates the view from nearly everywhere in the city.

Do I need a car in Colorado Springs? Yes, it is highly recommended. While some neighborhoods like Old Colorado City, Manitou Springs, and Downtown are very walkable within their boundaries, the top attractions are spread out. To efficiently visit Garden of the Gods, Pikes Peak, Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, and other sights, you will need a car.

No matter which neighborhood you choose, you’re setting the stage for an unforgettable Colorado adventure. Each area offers a unique lens through which to experience the beauty and spirit of the Pikes Peak region.
