The Perfect 5-Day Colorado Springs Itinerary (Icons & Local Secrets)

2026-09-16 • 14 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / The Perfect 5-Day Colorado Springs Itinerary (Icons & Local Secrets)

Forget trying to cram the epic landscapes of Colorado Springs into a single weekend. You’ll leave feeling rushed, having only scratched the surface. To truly drink in the mountain air, conquer the trails, and discover the city’s quirky charm, you need five days.

This isn’t your average checklist. This is a strategic plan, grouping attractions by location to minimize drive time and maximize adventure. We’ll hit the must-see giants, but we’ll also detour to the spots that locals love. Here’s exactly how to spend five perfect days in Colorado Springs.

Day 1: The Red Rock Corridor - Garden of the Gods & Its Local Sibling

We’re starting with the main event, but with a strategy to avoid the worst of the crowds. The key to this area is to go early and pair the world-famous park with its equally stunning, but less-trafficked, neighbor.

Morning: Conquer Garden of the Gods

Don’t roll out of bed at 10 a.m. and expect a parking spot. The magic of Garden of the Gods is best experienced in the crisp morning air, before the tour buses arrive. Aim to be at the park by 8:30 a.m. at the latest.

Head straight for the main parking lot off Juniper Way Loop and tackle the Perkins Central Garden Trail. It’s a ridiculously easy 1.5-mile paved loop that puts you right at the base of the most iconic formations, like the “Kissing Camels.” It’s accessible for strollers and wheelchairs, making it perfect for everyone.

Local Tip: Once you’ve done the main loop, get back in your car and drive the full Juniper Way Loop and Garden Drive. Pull over at the various lots to explore smaller, unpaved trails. The Siamese Twins Trail offers a fantastic natural “window” framing Pikes Peak—a photo op that most visitors miss.

  • Address: 1805 N 30th St, Colorado Springs, CO 80904
  • Cost: Absolutely free!

Lunch: Refuel in Old Colorado City

You’re just a five-minute drive from the historic heart of the region. Old Colorado City (OCC) is lined with Victorian buildings housing art galleries, boutiques, and fantastic restaurants. Skip the chain restaurants and grab a gourmet wood-fired pizza from Pizzeria Rustica. Their Napoletana-style pies are the real deal.

Afternoon: Hike Red Rock Canyon Open Space

This is the local’s alternative to Garden of the Gods. Located just south of Highway 24, Red Rock Canyon Open Space offers a similar landscape of sandstone “fins” and canyons but with a fraction of the people. It’s a former quarry, and you can still see remnants of its industrial past.

The trails here are more rugged and extensive, making it a better choice for a real hike. The 2.5-mile Red Rock Canyon Trail is a great loop that gives you a tour of the park’s best features, including a beautiful pond and sweeping views of the city. It’s also incredibly dog-friendly.

  • Address: 3550 W High St, Colorado Springs, CO 80904
  • Cost: Free.

Evening: Dinner and Drinks on the Westside

Stick around Old Colorado City for dinner. For a memorable meal, head to TAPAteria for Spanish-inspired small plates and a fantastic wine list. It’s the perfect way to cap off a day of exploring Colorado’s incredible geology.

Day 2: Altitude & Attitude - Pikes Peak & Manitou Springs

Today is a day of contrasts, from the 14,115-foot summit of “America’s Mountain” to the delightfully strange and colorful streets of Manitou Springs at its base.

Morning: Ascend Pikes Peak

You have two main options for reaching the summit: driving the Pikes Peak Highway or taking the Broadmoor Manitou and Pikes Peak Cog Railway.

  • Driving: The 19-mile paved road is a stunning journey, but not for the faint of heart. There are steep drop-offs and more than 150 turns. Make sure your car is in good condition, and take your time. You’ll need to pay a per-person toll.
  • Cog Railway: This is the stress-free option. The scenic 3.5-hour round trip is comfortable and offers narration along the way. Crucial Tip: You absolutely must book your tickets weeks, if not months, in advance, especially for a summer trip. They sell out fast.

Whichever way you go, the view from the top is breathtaking. Walk around the new Summit Visitor Center and, yes, you have to try their world-famous high-altitude donuts. Are they the best donuts you’ll ever have? Probably not. But eating a donut at 14,115 feet is a story you’ll tell forever.

Afternoon: Get Weird in Manitou Springs

After descending from the peak, dive into the wonderfully weird town of Manitou Springs. Park your car (you’ll likely have to pay) and just wander. The main drag is packed with art galleries, metaphysical shops, and arcades. Don’t miss the Manitou Springs Penny Arcade, which has a mix of vintage games from the 1930s and modern classics.

Be sure to sample the town’s namesake—the eight naturally carbonated mineral springs. Each has a unique taste. Bring a water bottle and try to find your favorite.

Evening: Soak and Recover

A day at high altitude followed by lots of walking will leave you tired. This is when having a great home base is critical. After grabbing an easy dinner at a Manitou spot like The Loop Mexican Restaurant, it’s time to relax.

For larger groups, a place like the Stunning Mountain Views, Hot Tub, Game Room is an absolute game-changer. Imagine recounting your Pikes Peak adventure while soaking in a private hot tub with those same mountains in the distance. Having a game room to keep the kids (or adults) entertained makes the evening that much better. This is what a Colorado Springs vacation rental is all about.

Day 3: Wildlife & Waterfalls - Cheyenne Mountain & The Broadmoor

Today, we explore the southern side of the city, home to America’s only mountain zoo and a set of falls so grand a world-class resort was built around them.

Morning: Cheyenne Mountain Zoo

This is not your average zoo. Built on the side of a mountain, the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo offers incredible views of the city below and a workout for your legs. The enclosures are designed to feel natural, and the layout encourages exploration.

The absolute must-do experience is feeding the giraffes. The herd is one of the largest in North America, and you can get eye-to-eye with these gentle giants on an elevated platform. Insider Tip: Buy more lettuce than you think you need; the experience is so fun you won’t want it to end. Book your zoo admission tickets online in advance to secure your spot.

  • Address: 4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd, Colorado Springs, CO 80906

Lunch: A Quick Bite

There are cafes inside the zoo, but you can also pack a lunch and enjoy it at one of the many scenic picnic spots. Alternatively, drive a few minutes down the mountain to Sacred Ground Cafe for excellent sandwiches and coffee.

Afternoon: The Seven Falls

Owned and operated by The Broadmoor, The Broadmoor Seven Falls is a series of seven cascading waterfalls in a 1,250-foot-wall box canyon. You’ll have to park at the Broadmoor and take a dedicated shuttle to the entrance.

Once there, you face a choice: climb the 224 steep steps alongside the falls or take the in-mountain elevator to the “Eagle’s Nest” viewing platform. If you’re able, the climb is worth it for the up-close views and photo ops. At the top, you’ll find short hiking trails that lead to even more panoramic vistas.

Evening: Experience The Broadmoor

You don’t have to be a guest to experience the grandeur of The Broadmoor resort. After your Seven Falls adventure, have the shuttle drop you back at the main hotel. Stroll around the picturesque lake, browse the high-end shops, and soak in the historic, European-inspired architecture.

For a splurge, have dinner at one of the resort’s many restaurants. For something more casual but still elegant, grab a classic cocktail and an appetizer at the Hotel Bar. It’s a taste of luxury and a perfect end to the day.

Day 4: Caves & History on the Westside

Today is all about the unique attractions clustered on the city’s west side, just west of I-25. It’s a mix of natural wonders, preserved history, and thrilling adventures.

Morning: Manitou Cliff Dwellings

Start your day with a trip back in time. The Manitou Cliff Dwellings are a fascinating architectural marvel. While these specific structures were relocated here in the early 1900s to preserve them, the materials and design are authentic Ancestral Puebloan.

You can walk through the multi-story dwellings at your own pace, imagining life here centuries ago. It’s a quick, educational, and visually impressive stop that’s great for all ages.

  • Address: 10 Cliff Dwellings Rd, Manitou Springs, CO 80829

Late Morning/Lunch: Ghost Town Museum

Lean into the Old West kitsch at the Ghost Town Museum. This indoor attraction is a preserved slice of a historic Colorado town, complete with a saloon, general store, and blacksmith shop. You can try your hand at a crank-operated butter churn or take aim in the shooting gallery. It’s a fun, quirky stop, especially on a day with less-than-perfect weather. Grab lunch nearby at a classic spot like Mo’s Diner & Lounge.

Afternoon: Cave of the Winds Mountain Park

Prepare for adventure. Cave of the Winds offers two main cave tours: the easier Discovery Tour, which is great for families, and the more adventurous Lantern Tour, where you explore darker, undeveloped passageways by candlelight.

But the fun doesn’t stop underground. Outside, you’ll find a host of thrill rides perched on the edge of Williams Canyon. The TERROR-dactyl launches you 200 feet into the canyon at nearly 100 miles per hour. It’s an adrenaline rush with an unbeatable view.

Evening: Westside Relaxation

The west side of Colorado Springs, especially the area around Old Colorado City, is packed with character. Staying nearby makes it easy to access all these attractions without constantly battling traffic.

A home like the Views | 2 mins to OCC | Fire Pit | Games | Pets is the perfect basecamp for this side of town. Being just two minutes from Old Colorado City means you can easily pop out for dinner or a morning coffee. After a day of exploring caves and canyons, gathering around a private fire pit to plan the next day’s adventure is the ideal way to unwind.

Day 5: A Grand Finale - The Royal Gorge or Downtown Discoveries

For your last day, you have a choice. Do you go on one more epic day trip, or do you stay local and discover the urban heart of Colorado Springs?

Option A: The Royal Gorge Bridge & Park (Full Day Trip)

If you have the time and the thirst for more jaw-dropping views, the Royal Gorge is worth the drive. Located about an hour southwest of Colorado Springs near Cañon City, it’s home to one of the world’s highest suspension bridges, hanging 956 feet above the Arkansas River.

Walk across the bridge, ride the aerial gondola for 360-degree views, and if you’re brave, ride the Cloudscraper Zip Line or the Royal Rush Skycoaster. It’s a full-day commitment but an unforgettable experience.

  • Address: 4218 Co Rd 3A, Cañon City, CO 81212

Option B: Urban Exploration (Local Day)

If you’d rather stay closer to home, spend your final day exploring Downtown Colorado Springs. Start with a visit to the impressive U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum, an interactive and inspiring tribute to Team USA.

Afterward, wander through Acacia Park, grab a craft coffee at The Wild Goose Meeting House, and browse the unique shops along Tejon Street. It’s a relaxed way to see a different side of the city. For a final hike, the trails at Palmer Park offer surprisingly rugged terrain and fantastic city views right in the middle of town.

Farewell Dinner

No matter which adventure you choose, end your trip with a fantastic meal. For a unique experience, try The Rabbit Hole, a whimsical underground restaurant with a creative New American menu. Or for something cozy and eclectic, Shuga’s is a long-time local favorite known for its spicy shrimp soup and craft cocktails.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best time of year to visit Colorado Springs?

Summer (June-August) offers the warmest weather and full access to all attractions, including the Pikes Peak Highway. However, it’s also the busiest. Fall (September-October) is a fantastic alternative, with pleasant temperatures, fewer crowds, and beautiful autumn colors in the mountains.

Do I need to worry about altitude sickness in Colorado Springs?

The city itself sits at an elevation of 6,035 feet. Most visitors acclimate quickly, but it’s wise to take it easy on your first day. The most important thing is to drink significantly more water than you usually do. When ascending Pikes Peak to over 14,000 feet, be aware that you may feel lightheaded or short of breath. Move slowly and listen to your body.

Is Colorado Springs expensive to visit?

It can be, but there are many ways to manage your budget. Many of the best natural attractions, like Garden of the Gods and Red Rock Canyon, are completely free. Choosing a Colorado Springs vacation rental allows you to cook meals, which saves a lot compared to dining out three times a day. When you book direct, you also avoid the extra fees charged by third-party travel sites.

How many days do you really need in Colorado Springs?

While you can see a few highlights in a weekend, it will feel very rushed. A 5-day itinerary like this one is ideal. It allows you to see the major sights at a comfortable pace, explore different neighborhoods, and even take a day trip without feeling like you’re constantly on the move.
