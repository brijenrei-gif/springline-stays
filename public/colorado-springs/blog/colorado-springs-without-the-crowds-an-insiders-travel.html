Colorado Springs Without the Crowds: An Insider's Travel Guide

2026-07-19 • 11 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / Colorado Springs Without the Crowds: An Insider's Travel Guide

Everyone has seen the photos: fiery red rocks of Garden of the Gods against a snow-capped Pikes Peak. They’re stunning. What the photos don’t show is the line of cars snaking through the park or the hunt for a parking spot that feels like an Olympic sport. Colorado Springs is magnificent, but its popularity means you’re often sharing that magnificence with thousands of your newest friends.

But it doesn’t have to be that way.

You can experience the awe-inspiring landscapes, the charming mountain towns, and the unforgettable adventures without the stress and the queues. It just takes a bit of local knowledge—knowing when to go, where to park, and which trail to take. This is your insider’s guide to seeing the best of Colorado Springs, but without the crowds.

Conquering Garden of the Gods (The Smart Way)

Garden of the Gods is a National Natural Landmark for a reason—it’s breathtaking. But the main loop road can feel like a parking lot from 10 AM to 3 PM, especially on a summer weekend. Here’s how to outsmart the masses.

H3: Timing is Everything: The Sunrise & Sunset Secret

This is the single most important tip. Set your alarm. Seeing the first rays of sun hit the Kissing Camels rock formation, turning it a brilliant orange, is an experience you’ll never forget. At sunrise, you’ll share the park with a few photographers, trail runners, and maybe a wandering mule deer—not busloads of tourists. The same goes for the “golden hour” before sunset. The light is magical, the temperatures are cooler, and the parking lots have miraculously emptied out.

Pro Tip: Pack a thermos of coffee for a sunrise mission or a blanket and snacks for a sunset picnic. There are plenty of pull-offs with benches to sit and just soak it all in.

H3: Ditch the Main Lot: Use a Lesser-Known Entrance

Most visitors enter off 30th Street and head straight for the main parking lot near the North Main/Perkins Central Garden Trail. This is chaos. Instead, act like a local and use one of the other entrances.

My personal favorite is the entrance off Gateway Road on the south side of the park. From here, you have immediate access to parking for the Siamese Twins Trail—a fantastic, short loop that perfectly frames Pikes Peak through a natural rock window. It’s one of the best photo ops in the entire park, and it’s far less crowded than the main loop.

Another great option is the Rock Ledge Ranch Historic Site entrance. This gives you easy access to the western side of the park and trails that are often much quieter.

H3: Go Beyond the Paved Path

The 1.5-mile Perkins Central Garden Trail is paved and accessible, which also means it’s the most crowded. It’s absolutely worth seeing, but the real magic happens when you step onto the dirt trails.

  • Chambers/Bretag/Palmer Trail: This is a fantastic 3-mile loop that circles the entire main rock formation area. It offers incredible, ever-changing views, has some moderate inclines to get your heart rate up, and is significantly less traveled than the inner loop.
  • Siamese Twins Trail: As mentioned, this 0.5-mile loop is a must-do for the iconic Pikes Peak photo. It’s easy, quick, and delivers a huge scenic reward.
  • Ridge Trail: A short but slightly more strenuous 0.5-mile loop with some scrambling that rewards you with elevated views away from the crowds on the main path.

Pikes Peak or Bust (Without the Gridlock)

Reaching the 14,115-foot summit of “America’s Mountain” is a core part of the Colorado Springs experience. The views that inspired the song “America the Beautiful” are unparalleled. But choosing how and when you go up makes all the difference.

H3: The Cog Railway vs. Driving: An Honest Comparison

This is the big question for most visitors. Here’s the breakdown:

The Broadmoor Manitou and Pikes Peak Cog Railway: * Pros: Absolutely zero stress. You get to sit back, relax, and enjoy the stunning scenery without worrying about hairpin turns, mountain driving, or parking at the top. The narration is informative, and the journey itself is an iconic experience. * Cons: It’s expensive, and you must book tickets far in advance, especially during peak season. You’re also on their schedule, with a limited amount of time at the summit (usually around 40 minutes). * Verdict: Best for families with young children, those nervous about mountain driving, or anyone who wants a guaranteed, stress-free ascent.

Driving the Pikes Peak Highway: * Pros: Total freedom. You can stop at any of the numerous pull-offs, lakes, and picnic areas on your own schedule. It’s also more cost-effective for a car full of people than individual train tickets. You can spend as much time as you want at the summit. * Cons: It can be a nerve-wracking drive for the uninitiated. The road is steep with sharp drop-offs. During peak times, you can get stuck in a long line of cars, and parking at the summit can be a challenge. Important: From late May to September, a timed entry reservation is now required to drive to the summit, in addition to the gate fee. * Verdict: Best for confident drivers, adventurers who want to explore at their own pace, and those looking to save a bit of money.

Insider’s Tip: If you choose to drive, go early. Arrive at the toll gate right when it opens (usually 7:30 AM) to beat the rush and enjoy a clearer road and a less-crowded summit. And don’t forget to grab one of their world-famous high-altitude donuts at the Summit Visitor Center!

Manitou Springs: Finding Charm Amidst the Chaos

The historic, quirky town of Manitou Springs, nestled at the base of Pikes Peak, is a must-visit. Its streets are lined with art galleries, unique boutiques, and vintage arcades. But its narrow roads and limited parking can make for a frustrating visit on a busy Saturday.

H3: The Parking Predicament and How to Solve It

Don’t even try to find a spot on Manitou Avenue in the middle of the day. You’ll just circle endlessly. The secret is to park at the free lot at 10 Old Man’s Trail and take the free shuttle (Route 33) into the heart of town. It runs frequently and drops you right in the middle of the action, saving you a massive headache.

H3: Weekday Afternoons are Your Best Friend

If your schedule allows, visit Manitou on a weekday. The town has a completely different, more relaxed vibe. You can browse the shops without bumping elbows and get a table at a great restaurant without a long wait. Tuesday or Wednesday afternoons are the sweet spot for a peaceful experience.

H3: Sip the Springs, Skip the Crowded Restaurants

Part of the town’s history revolves around its eight naturally carbonated mineral springs. Grab a map from the visitor center and go on a self-guided tasting tour. Each spring has a unique taste due to its mineral content. It’s a fun, free activity that gets you walking through the less-crowded side streets. For food, instead of waiting for a table at a packed restaurant, grab a delicious slice from Savelli’s Pizza or a gourmet sandwich from Adam’s Mountain Cafe and find a quiet bench in a park to enjoy it.

Red Rock Canyon: The Local’s Garden of the Gods

Want the stunning red rock formations without the traffic? Head to Red Rock Canyon Open Space. Located just south of Highway 24, this city-owned park is a 1,474-acre paradise that many tourists completely miss.

H3: Why It’s a Fantastic Alternative

While it doesn’t have the same towering, dramatic spires as Garden of the Gods, it offers a more expansive and less-structured experience. You’ll find a huge network of trails, a beautiful quarry-turned-pond, and plenty of space to roam. It’s also incredibly dog-friendly, with designated off-leash areas.

H3: Best Trails for Views and Solitude

  • Contemplative Trail: This is an easy, 1.3-mile loop that winds through a former quarry. It’s a serene walk with beautiful rock walls and is perfect for a quiet afternoon.
  • Red Rock Canyon Trail to Section 16: For a more challenging hike with a huge payoff, connect from the main park trails up to Section 16. The climb is strenuous, but the panoramic views of Garden of the Gods, Pikes Peak, and the city are absolutely worth it. You’ll leave 99% of the park visitors behind.

The main parking lot is located at 3550 W High St, Colorado Springs, CO 80904.

Frequently Asked Questions

H3: What is the least crowded time to visit Colorado Springs?

The “shoulder seasons”—typically September/October and April/May—are fantastic. You’ll experience pleasant weather, fall colors or spring blooms, and significantly fewer crowds than in the peak summer months of June through August. Visiting mid-week (Tuesday-Thursday) will always be less crowded than on weekends.

H3: Is it better to drive or take the Cog Railway up Pikes Peak?

It depends on your priorities. The Cog Railway is a stress-free, iconic experience but is more expensive and runs on a fixed schedule. Driving offers flexibility and is more budget-friendly for groups but requires a timed reservation in summer and confident mountain driving skills.

H3: How can I avoid traffic at Garden of the Gods?

The best way is to go very early in the morning (sunrise) or later in the evening (an hour or two before sunset). Use a secondary entrance like the one on Gateway Road instead of the main entrance on 30th Street to bypass the initial bottleneck.

H3: What is a good, less-crowded alternative to Garden of the Gods?

Red Rock Canyon Open Space is the local’s choice. It offers similar beautiful red rock formations, an extensive network of trails for hiking and biking, and a fraction of the visitors you’ll find at Garden of the Gods.

Planning a trip to Colorado Springs is exciting, and with a few insider tips, you can trade the traffic jams and crowded trails for peaceful sunrises and wide-open spaces. Your adventure doesn’t have to be a battle with the crowds.
