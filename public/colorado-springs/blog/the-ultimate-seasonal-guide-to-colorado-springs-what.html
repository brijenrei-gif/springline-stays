The Ultimate Seasonal Guide to Colorado Springs: What to Do Year-Round

2026-09-10 • 12 min read
Home / Colorado Springs Stays / The Ultimate Seasonal Guide to Colorado Springs: What to Do Year-Round

Forget everything you think you know about “mountain towns.” Colorado Springs isn’t just a summer hiking destination or a winter ski stopover. This city transforms with the seasons, offering a completely different adventure every few months. The secret isn’t if you should visit, but when you should visit to have the exact experience you’re dreaming of.

Whether you’re chasing golden aspens in the fall or tackling the Manitou Incline in the summer sun, we’ve got the insider tips to make it happen. This is your definitive, season-by-season guide to making the most of Colorado Springs.

Colorado Springs in the Spring (March - May): The Awakening

Spring in Colorado Springs is a delightful tease. One day it’s sunny and 60°F, the next you’re getting a surprise snow squall. But that unpredictability is part of the charm. The crowds are thin, the trails are starting to thaw, and the city feels like it’s stretching after a long winter nap.

See the First Blooms at Garden of the Gods

While stunning year-round, the Garden of the Gods has a special magic in the spring. The iconic red rocks are contrasted with patches of bright green and the first resilient wildflowers.

  • Local Tip: Drive the Juniper Way Loop and pull off at the High Point Overlook for a panoramic view of the park with Pikes Peak in the background. The paved Perkins Central Garden Trail is perfect for a low-key stroll and is accessible for all. Go on a weekday morning to feel like you have the whole place to yourself.
  • Address: 1805 N 30th St, Colorado Springs, CO 80904

Tackle the Manitou Incline Before the Summer Rush

If you’re an avid hiker, spring is the prime time to conquer the legendary Manitou Incline. This grueling staircase of over 2,744 steps gains nearly 2,000 feet of elevation in less than a mile. In spring, the cooler temperatures make the ascent far more manageable than in the scorching summer heat.

  • Crucial Info: You must make a free reservation online in advance. They fill up, so book it as soon as you know your dates. The way down is the 4-mile Barr Trail—do not attempt to descend the Incline steps.
  • Address: Access via Ruxton Ave, Manitou Springs, CO 80829

Find a Patio in Old Colorado City

As the weather warms, the patios in historic Old Colorado City come to life. This charming district, once the original capital of the Colorado Territory, is lined with Victorian buildings housing art galleries, boutiques, and fantastic restaurants.

  • Food Recommendation: Grab a table on the patio at Paravicini’s Italian Bistro. Their Gnocchi Gorgonzola is the stuff of legends. For something more casual, the patio at Cerberus Brewing Company offers award-winning beers and incredible pub food.

Colorado Springs in the Summer (June - August): Peak Adventure Season

Summer is when Colorado Springs truly comes alive. The days are long and sunny, the high country is fully accessible, and the energy is electric. It’s the perfect time for grand adventures and making lifelong memories.

Reach the Summit of Pikes Peak

Seeing “America’s Mountain” from town is one thing; standing on its 14,115-foot summit is another. You have three main options to get to the top: 1. Drive the Pikes Peak Highway: A breathtaking (and winding) 19-mile scenic drive. Allow at least 2-3 hours for the round trip. Note: Timed entry reservations are often required. 2. Ride The Broadmoor’s Pikes Peak Cog Railway: A historic and comfortable 3.5-hour round trip that takes you right to the summit. Book tickets well in advance, as they sell out. 3. Hike It: For the truly adventurous, the 13-mile (one-way) Barr Trail is a legendary challenge. This is an all-day affair for experienced, acclimatized hikers only.

  • Don’t Miss: No matter how you get there, you have to try one of the world-famous high-altitude donuts at the Summit Visitor Center. They’re made with a special recipe that allows them to stay fluffy at 14,000 feet.

Cool Off at Seven Falls

Tucked into a 1,250-foot-wall box canyon, The Broadmoor Seven Falls is a series of seven cascading waterfalls with a total drop of 181 feet. You can climb the 224 steps alongside the falls for incredible views or take the in-mountain elevator to the “Eagle’s Nest” viewing platform.

  • Address: 1045 Lower Gold Camp Rd, Colorado Springs, CO 80905
  • Pro Tip: Your ticket also includes access to the trails at the top of the falls. The short hike to Midnight Falls is absolutely worth it for a more secluded and serene experience away from the main crowds.

Go Wild at the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo

America’s only mountain zoo is a must-do, especially for families. The enclosures are built right into the mountainside, offering incredible views of the city below. The absolute highlight is feeding the giraffes—the herd is one of the largest and most prolific in the world, and they’ll eat right out of your hand.

  • Address: 4250 Cheyenne Mountain Zoo Rd, Colorado Springs, CO 80906

After a long summer day of exploring, a place to unwind is essential. For larger families or groups, a spacious home base makes all the difference.

The Stunning Mountain Views, Hot Tub, Game Room home sleeps up to 11 and is the perfect retreat. Imagine soaking in the private hot tub after conquering the Incline or challenging the family to a pool tournament in the game room. This Colorado Springs vacation rental, located at 26 Westmark, provides the perfect amenities to recharge for another day of adventure.

Colorado Springs in the Fall (September - November): Golden Hues & Cool Vibes

Ask any local, and they’ll likely tell you that autumn is the best season in Colorado. The summer crowds have thinned, the air is crisp and cool, and the landscape explodes in a riot of gold, orange, and red.

Drive Gold Camp Road for Aspen Views

While the Pikes Peak Highway gets a lot of attention, the historic Gold Camp Road is the local’s choice for fall colors. This unpaved road follows an old railroad grade, winding through tunnels and offering spectacular views of changing aspen groves. The stretch from Colorado Springs to Victor is a classic scenic drive.

  • Local Secret: For an even better experience, bike it! The gentle grade and lack of traffic on certain sections make it a fantastic and immersive way to see the aspens up close.

Get Spooked in Manitou Springs

Historic and quirky Manitou Springs leans into its haunted reputation every October. You can take guided ghost tours through the historic district, hearing tales of the town’s paranormal residents. The whole town gets decorated, making it a fun and festive place to stroll, shop, and grab a bite.

  • Don’t Miss: The annual Emma Crawford Coffin Races. Yes, you read that right. Teams dress up in costumes and race down the main street pushing a “coffin” with their own “Emma” inside. It’s a bizarre and quintessentially Colorado event you won’t forget.

Hike Among the Hoodoos at Red Rock Canyon

While beautiful in spring, Red Rock Canyon Open Space takes on a different personality in the fall. The scrub oak turns a deep rust color, creating a stunning contrast with the red rock formations. The cooler weather is perfect for exploring the network of trails.

  • Trail Recommendation: The Contemplative Trail to the Red Rock Canyon Quarry is a fantastic hike that offers a bit of history and incredible views back toward Garden of the Gods.
  • Address: 3550 W High St, Colorado Springs, CO 80904

Cool fall evenings are made for gathering around a fire. It’s the perfect way to end a day of exploring Old Colorado City or hiking through golden forests.

The Views | 2 mins to OCC | Fire Pit | Games | Pets property is an ideal autumn escape. Located at 1112 Arch St, it’s just a stone’s throw from Old Colorado City. After dinner, you can head back to your private yard, light up the fire pit, and roast some s’mores under the stars. It’s a pet-friendly spot, so your four-legged friend can join in on the fall fun.

Colorado Springs in the Winter (December - February): A Cozy Mountain Wonderland

While Colorado Springs doesn’t get the same massive snow dumps as the high-country ski resorts, it offers a milder, sunnier winter experience with plenty of its own charm. The snow-dusted red rocks and Pikes Peak create a picturesque backdrop for cozy and festive activities.

Skate Downtown at Acacia Park

Each winter, a portion of Acacia Park in the heart of downtown is transformed into a charming outdoor ice rink. Twinkling lights, music, and the city skyline make for a magical experience. It’s the perfect family-friendly activity or a romantic date night.

  • Address: 115 E Platte Ave, Colorado Springs, CO 80903
  • Tip: Go in the evening to skate under the lights, then walk over to one of downtown’s many amazing restaurants for dinner.

Marvel at The Broadmoor’s Holiday Lights

The historic Broadmoor hotel goes all-out for the holidays. Starting in late November, the entire property is decked out in millions of twinkling lights. You don’t have to be a hotel guest to visit. Stroll around the lake, admire the massive gingerbread house in the main building, and soak in the festive atmosphere. It’s a beloved local tradition.

Try Cross-Country Skiing or Snowshoeing

For a true winter adventure, head 45 minutes up the road to Mueller State Park. With over 50 miles of trails and stunning views of the western slope of Pikes Peak, it’s a paradise for snowshoeing and cross-country skiing. The trails are well-marked and offer options for all skill levels.

On those truly cold or snowy days, having a vacation rental with incredible indoor amenities is a game-changer. The Epic Family Home: Mini-Golf, Sauna, Hot Tub, Games is an entertainment destination in itself. Located at 19 Clover, this home ensures a “snow day” is the best day of your trip. Challenge the family to a round on the private indoor mini-golf course, warm up in the sauna after a day in the snow, or relax in the hot tub. It’s an unbeatable option for a winter family getaway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the best month to visit Colorado Springs?

A: It truly depends on what you want to do. September is arguably the best all-around month, with pleasant temperatures, fewer crowds, and stunning fall foliage. July and August are best for high-elevation hiking and water activities, while December and January offer a festive, cozy winter experience.

Q: How many days do you need in Colorado Springs?

A: To see the main highlights like Garden of the Gods, Pikes Peak, and Manitou Springs without feeling rushed, plan for at least 3 to 4 full days. If you want to add more hiking, day trips, or explore different neighborhoods, a 5 to 7-day trip is ideal.

Q: Is Colorado Springs better in summer or winter?

A: Neither is “better,” they just offer different experiences. Summer is for peak adventure: all trails and roads are open, and the city is bustling with energy. Winter is quieter, cozier, and more picturesque, with opportunities for snow sports and festive holiday activities. The “shoulder seasons” of spring and fall offer a great balance of pleasant weather and fewer tourists.

Q: Do I need a car in Colorado Springs?

A: Yes, it is highly recommended. While you can use ride-sharing services for getting around the downtown core and Old Colorado City, many of the top attractions like Pikes Peak, Mueller State Park, and trailheads are spread out and not accessible by public transport. A car gives you the freedom to explore at your own pace.

No matter what season you choose, Colorado Springs is ready to impress. The key is to plan your trip around the unique adventures each time of year offers.
